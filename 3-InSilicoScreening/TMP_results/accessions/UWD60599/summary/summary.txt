Paper: PMC13189575.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- No temperature-related data was found in the provided text.

Environmental Context:
- No environmental context related to temperature was found in the provided text.

Concise Summary:
This systematic review analyzed the patterns of medicine information (MI) receipt among adult medicine users in developed economies between 2010 and 2025. The study found that traditional sources, such as physicians, pharmacists, and package leaflets (PLs), remain the most common MI sources, despite the growing role of electronic platforms like the internet and social media. No clear temporal trends in MI receipt were observed. The review highlighted that MI receipt from electronic sources is more common among internet-experienced users, but the literature lacks differentiation between various digital MI subtypes (e.g., websites vs. social media vs. AI-based tools). The analysis noted that the heterogeneity across study designs, populations, and outcomes precluded a meta-analysis, necessitating a narrative synthesis. Key gaps identified include the need for more theory-driven research, better differentiation of digital MI sources, and longitudinal, population-level studies to track MI trends accurately.
