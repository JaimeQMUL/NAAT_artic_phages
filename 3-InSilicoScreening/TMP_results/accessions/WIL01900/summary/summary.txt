Concise Summary:

The study focused on enhancing phage stability through encapsulation to withstand acidic and simulated gut stresses, rather than characterizing inherent thermal adaptation of the phages themselves.

Paper: PMC13147348.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37°C
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but optimal growth was at 37°C.
- Survival temperature range: Tested survival at 4°C, 25°C, 37°C, and 45°C (storage); Heat resistance tested at 70°C, 80°C, and 95°C (feed processing).
- Incubation conditions: 37°C for 24h (initial culture); 37°C for 48h (sporulation).
- Thermophilic adaptation: One strain originated from a thermophilic hot spring habitat.
- Heat tolerance: Spores maintained high survival rates at 80°C and 95°C when coencapsulated with the polysaccharide matrix, demonstrating suitability for feed processing temperatures (70°C–95°C).
- Cold tolerance: Viability was tested at 4°C for extended storage periods (up to 60 days).

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolated from diverse ecological niches, including shrimp pond soil, hot spring soil, chicken manure, and rubber latex serum. One strain was explicitly noted as originating from a thermophilic hot spring habitat.

Concise Summary:
The probiotic consortium, composed of four spore-forming strains, was cultured optimally at 37°C. The strains were isolated from varied environments, including one strain sourced from a thermophilic hot spring habitat. The study extensively tested thermal resilience, showing that coencapsulation with the polysaccharide matrix significantly enhanced spore survival during simulated heat treatments up to 95°C, confirming suitability for commercial feed processing temperatures. Furthermore, the encapsulated spores demonstrated viability maintenance across a range of storage temperatures, including 4°C, 25°C, 37°C, and 45°C, with superior stability observed in the polysaccharide-enhanced formulation compared to alginate-only encapsulation. The overall evidence supports the strains' robustness across mesophilic (37°C) and elevated thermal stress conditions.
