Environmental Context:
- The samples analyzed originated from diverse environmental sources, including nearshore waters of a large freshwater lake and general environmental metagenomic datasets.

Concise Summary:
The paper describes a computational pipeline (PhagePhisher) designed for the discovery and extraction of viral sequences (bacteriophages, prophages) from complex, mixed genomic datasets obtained via whole genome sequencing (WGS) and metagenomics. The methodology is computational, relying on a subtractive approach to filter out host/background DNA reads. The pipeline was successfully tested on three proof-of-concept datasets: 1) a phage isolated alongside its host genome; 2) a cyanophage from a highly contaminated sample; and 3) a metagenomic sample containing various lytic and lysogenic phages from a freshwater lake environment. The analysis confirms the tool's utility in resolving viral signals, even in 'hard-to-sequence' or highly contaminated samples, and can be applied to both bacterial and eukaryotic viral systems. No physiological data regarding temperature optima or thermal adaptation was reported.

Paper: PMC10947687.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperatures were reported in the range of 22–25°C for two clades.
- The experimental temperature range used was 16–27°C.
- The observed thermal optimum for one strain was 18°C, differing from previously reported optima (25°C).
- The study found weak physiological effects of temperature alone on cell quotas and stoichiometry under nutrient-replete conditions.
- The authors noted that temperature effects on growth rate were only observed when strain identity was also considered, suggesting an indirect thermal effect.
- The lack of strong direct thermal effects was attributed to the experimental conditions not probing the lower thermal limits of the strains.

Environmental Context:
- The strains were isolated from various locations, including the California Current, Balearic Sea, and English Channel.
- The strains were characterized as having different adaptations to environmental nutrients (e.g., mesotrophic vs. oligotrophic).

Concise Summary:
Analysis of four closely related strains revealed that while the strains exhibit distinct clades and adaptations, the study found that intraspecific variation in traits like cell size and elemental quotas was a stronger determinant of stoichiometry than temperature alone. The optimal growth temperatures reported for the strains were generally in the 22–25°C range, though one strain showed an observed optimum of 18°C under the experimental conditions (16–27°C). The authors noted that the lack of a strong direct thermal effect on stoichiometry was likely due to the experimental design not testing the lower thermal limits of the organisms. The strains were sourced from diverse marine environments and displayed varying adaptations to nutrient availability (e.g., mesotrophic vs. oligotrophic). The study emphasizes that strain identity and phylogeny play a major role in the stoichiometric response, suggesting that considering intraspecific trait variation is crucial for accurate biogeochemical modeling, even when thermal effects are weak or indirect.
