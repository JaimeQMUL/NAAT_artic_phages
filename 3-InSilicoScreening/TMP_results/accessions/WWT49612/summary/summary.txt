Paper: PMC13153029.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Stable from 4 °C to 50 °C.
- Optimal activity observed at 37 °C (physiological temperature).
- Stability maintained at 4 °C for over a year (long-term storage).
- Stability declined after 80 min at 60 °C, with an 87.8% reduction in titer.
- Rapid decline observed at 70 °C.
- Stable within the temperature range of 4 °C−50 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from untreated sewage at a hospital.

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibits robust thermal stability, maintaining activity across a broad range from 4 °C to 50 °C. It shows optimal activity at 37 °C, consistent with physiological conditions, and demonstrates excellent long-term storage stability at 4 °C. Thermal inactivation begins to occur significantly above 50 °C, with notable decline observed at 60 °C and rapid loss of titer at 70 °C. The phage was isolated from untreated sewage, suggesting adaptation to a complex, potentially variable environmental matrix, though no explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was provided by the authors.
