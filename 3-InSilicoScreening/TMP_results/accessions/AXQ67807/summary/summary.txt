Concise Summary:
Protocatechuic acid production and gene expression were analyzed across three temperatures: 25 °C, 30 °C, and 37 °C. Catechol production was highest at 25 °C, suggesting optimal conditions for this compound at ambient temperatures. Gene expression of the relevant enzyme (asbF) was also lowest at 37 °C, indicating that the compound's regulation is favored at lower temperatures, consistent with its hypothesized role in soil growth cycles. The organism is an ubiquitous soil bacterium, and the experimental design explicitly compared conditions mimicking soil environments (lower temperatures) against human virulence temperatures (37 °C). No explicit classification (e.g., mesophile) was provided, but the data strongly suggest adaptation and optimal function in cooler, ambient conditions relative to the tested pathogenic temperature.
