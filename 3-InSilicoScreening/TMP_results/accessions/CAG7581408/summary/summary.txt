Concise Summary:
This study focuses on the auxiliary metabolic gene *pebS*, an enzyme found in cyanophages that catalyzes the reduction of biliverdin IX$\alpha$ to phycoerythrobilin (PEB). The analysis compares this phage-encoded enzyme to the two-step reduction pathway utilized by the host cyanobacteria, which employ two distinct enzymes (*PebA* and *PebB*). The research utilized comparative genomics, screening the Ocean Gene Atlas and NCBI cyanobacterial genomes. Phylogenetic analysis confirmed that *pebS*, *PebA*, and *PebB* form distinct clades. Crucially, the study concluded that *pebS* is exclusively encoded by phages, as no corresponding gene was found in the analyzed cyanobacterial genomes, which instead possess *PebA* and *PebB*. The detected *pebS* sequences in cyanobacterial assemblies were confirmed to originate from phage contigs, suggesting the samples were derived from lytic phage infections. The environmental context is strictly marine, involving photosynthetic cyanobacteria. No data regarding the thermal physiology or adaptation of the organisms was presented.
