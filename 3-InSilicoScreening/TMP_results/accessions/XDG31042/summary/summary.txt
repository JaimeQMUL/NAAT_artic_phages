Concise Summary:
This study analyzes the heterogeneous selection landscape of genome evolution across over 22,000 bacterial and archaeal genomes using phylogenetic methods. The core finding is a distinct dichotomy in evolutionary regimes between the two domains: most bacterial lineages show a positive correlation between genome size and selection strength, indicative of selection for functional diversification. In contrast, all analyzed archaeal lineages exhibit a strong negative correlation, consistent with genome streamlining. The authors conclude that prokaryote evolution reflects a continuum of selective regimes shaped by ecology and population genetics, rather than a single universal pattern. The analysis also noted that gene flux correlates positively with selective constraints, suggesting that intense genomic dynamism is associated with stronger selective pressures.
