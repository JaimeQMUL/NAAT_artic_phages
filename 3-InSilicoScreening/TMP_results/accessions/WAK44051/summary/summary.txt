Paper: PMC12031009.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for *C. perfringens* in milk/cheese; for *S. aureus* in milk/cheese).
- Growth temperature range: 25 °C–7 °C (for *S. aureus* in milk); 4 °C (for *L. monocytogenes* in fresh cheese); 24 °C (for *C. perfringens* in cheese slices); 25 °C (for *E. coli* in lettuce); 30 °C (for *A. hydrophila*); 37 °C (for *C. perfringens* in milk).
- Survival temperature range: 4 °C to 50 °C (for *C. perfringens* in lettuce); 4 °C to 95 °C (for chimeric endolysin against *C. perfringens*).
- Incubation conditions: 10 °C for 6 weeks (for *S. aureus* in cheese); 4 °C for 3 h (for *L. monocytogenes* in soy milk); 4 °C for 14 days (for *S. aureus* in cheese); 4 °C for 28 days (for *L. monocytogenes* in fresh cheese); 4 °C to 12 °C (for *S. aureus* in cheese); 42 °C (for *C. perfringens* in lettuce); 4 °C (for *C. perfringens* in duck meat); 25 °C (for *S. aureus* on ham/surfaces); 25 °C (for *E. coli* in lettuce).
- Thermophilic adaptation: None explicitly stated for the enzymes/organisms in the context of optimal growth, but stability up to 95 °C was noted for one chimeric endolysin.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: Activity observed at 4 °C (e.g., *L. monocytogenes* in fresh cheese; *S. aureus* in cheese storage).
- Mesophilic growth: General activity observed at 25 °C–37 °C.
- Heat tolerance: Stability maintained up to 50 °C (for *C. perfringens* in lettuce); activity decreased above 50 °C (for *C. perfringens* in lettuce).
- Cold tolerance: Activity observed at 4 °C (e.g., *L. monocytogenes* in fresh cheese).
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: Stability across a wide range (e.g., 4 °C to 95 °C for one chimeric endolysin).
- Membrane adaptations: Not explicitly detailed in relation to temperature adaptation.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Food matrices (milk, cheese, lettuce, beef, salmon, etc.); soil/water (general source for pathogens).

Concise Summary:
Endolysins demonstrate broad functional stability, with some engineered enzymes showing stability up to 95 °C. Optimal activity is frequently observed in the mesophilic range (25–37 °C) for pathogens like *S. aureus* and *C. perfringens*. Significant activity is documented at refrigeration temperatures (4 °C), particularly for *L. monocytogenes* and *S. aureus* in cheese matrices, suggesting psychrotolerance for biocontrol applications. The activity against *C. perfringens* in lettuce was effective at 42 °C, but activity decreased above 50 °C. The enzymes show adaptability across various food matrices and storage conditions, with some exhibiting prolonged activity over weeks at low temperatures. The literature highlights the potential for these enzymes to function effectively in both psychrophilic and mesophilic food preservation settings, although optimal conditions remain highly dependent on the specific food matrix and bacterial species.
