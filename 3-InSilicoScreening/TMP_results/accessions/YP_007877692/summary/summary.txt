Paper: PMC8938411.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- The protein exhibited a single transition point (melting temperature, TM) of 58 °C, consistent with a stable, folded entity.
- The thermal stability of the protein was significantly increased by the presence of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ (TM increased to 73 °C).
- The presence of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ and phosphate-embellished sugars (FBP) further increased thermal stability (TM reached 76 °C).
- The protein showed thermal stability changes across a thermal gradient during ligand screening.

Environmental Context:
- The organism is a marine picocyanobacterium.
- The protein's function is inferred to be related to nutrient acquisition in low-carbon environments, suggesting adaptation to nutrient-poor conditions.

Concise Summary:
Characterization of a sugar-binding protein from a marine picocyanobacterium revealed thermal stability data. The protein exhibits a baseline melting temperature of 58 °C. Its thermal stability is significantly enhanced by the binding of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ (TM $\approx$ 73 °C) and further stabilized by phosphate-embellished sugars like fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (TM $\approx$ 76 °C). These findings suggest the protein is adapted to function under conditions where metal ions and specific sugar moieties are present, consistent with its role in nutrient acquisition in marine, low-carbon environments. No explicit classification (e.g., mesophile, thermophile) was provided, but the stability data points to functional robustness in the marine environment.
