Paper: PMC10253784.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Stable up to 65 °C.
- Inactivated by heating at 75 °C (15 min) and 100 °C (30 min).
- Significant decrease in viability observed when exposed to temperatures between 25 °C and 65 °C for 15 or 30 minutes.
- Most stable temperature for storage was determined to be 4 °C compared to -20 °C and 25 °C after 5 months.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from pigeon feces in the United Arab Emirates (UAE).

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophage demonstrated stability up to 65 °C, though it was inactivated by heating at 75 °C for 15 minutes and 100 °C for 30 minutes. Viability showed a significant decline when exposed to temperatures ranging from 25 °C to 65 °C. For long-term storage, 4 °C was identified as the most stable temperature over a 5-month period. The phage was isolated from pigeon feces in the UAE. The authors did not provide an explicit classification (e.g., mesophile) but detailed its thermal stability profile for potential use in food processing and therapy.

Environmental Context:

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the antibacterial and antibiofilm potential of saponins extracted from the aerial parts of a plant species against multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria. The research focused on the chemical properties and bioactivity of the isolated compounds, showing modest antibacterial activity (MIC of 4 mg/mL) and strong antibiofilm potential (up to 75% inhibition by compound 3). Mechanistically, the compounds were found to upregulate efflux pump genes, and molecular docking confirmed the interaction of one compound with the adeB efflux pump. No information regarding the optimal, range, or adaptation to temperature extremes was presented.
