Environmental Context:
- No specific environmental source or habitat was detailed, other than growth on a substrate.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the interaction of phototropism and gravitropism in a cyanobacterium. The physiological mechanisms observed relate to the directional sensing of light, adhesion, and external physical forces (including gravity). Pigment distribution (phycobilins and chlorophyll) is regulated by these directional cues, with phycobilins targeting the light-proximal membrane and chlorophyll localizing opposite the external force. The mechanism involves polyphosphate bodies, and force application affects colonial morphology. The paper reports the first known intracellular directional gravity response mechanism in a prokaryote, but provides no data regarding temperature optima, ranges, or thermal adaptations.
