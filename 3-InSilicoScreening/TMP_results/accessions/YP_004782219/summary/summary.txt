- The organism is a thermophile, and the regulatory system is described as a conserved, temperature-responsive regulatory paradigm in its genus.

Concise Summary:
The study characterizes a novel, temperature-sensitive two-component system (TCS) in a thermophilic bacterium. The system is designed to sense thermal cues and orchestrate cellular thermoadaptation, specifically mediating a response to cold stress (50°C). The sensor histidine kinase component exhibits high thermostability, maintaining activity up to 90°C, with optimal activity observed at 70°C. The TCS utilizes a unique, noncanonical phosphotransfer mechanism, transferring a phosphate group from the histidine residue of the kinase to a tyrosine residue (Tyr84) on the response regulator, deviating from the typical aspartate-based phosphorelay. Functionally, this system directly regulates the expression of a key enzyme in the shikimate pathway (DHQase), which is essential for aromatic amino acid biosynthesis. Under cold stress, the activation of this TCS leads to the upregulation of DHQase, ensuring metabolic flux necessary for cold adaptation. The system also exhibits a negative feedback loop, with the response regulator repressing its own expression. The overall regulatory circuit demonstrates an elegant coupling between environmental sensing (temperature) and core metabolism, providing a model for microbial environmental adaptation in thermophiles.

Concise Summary:
This study investigates the ultrafast electronic and vibrational dynamics of blue copper sites in two evolutionarily divergent proteins: one single-domain electron-transfer protein and one two-domain multicopper oxidase. The research utilized transient absorption spectroscopy to compare the excited-state dynamics of these proteins, focusing on low-frequency collective modes. The analysis revealed that the protein fold surrounding the conserved copper coordination sphere significantly modulates these ultrafast energy relaxation pathways. Specifically, the single-domain protein exhibited a low-frequency mode at 29 cm⁻¹, while the multicopper oxidase displayed a mode at 38 cm⁻¹. The authors correlated these differences in vibrational dynamics with the structural rigidity imparted by the protein scaffold, suggesting that protein mechanics control the ultrafast nuclear dynamics at the active site. No information regarding the thermal adaptation, growth temperature, or environmental source extremes of the studied proteins was provided.
