Paper: PMC9319786.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for bacterial growth and phage activity).
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but optimal activity observed at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Stable at 4 °C and -70 °C (with or without glycerol) over six months. Activity was completely lost at 37 °C after 6 months of storage, suggesting high sensitivity to prolonged incubation at body temperature.
- Incubation conditions: Optimal lytic activity observed at 37 °C for bacterial lysis and phage amplification. Phage stability was tested at 37 °C, 22–24 °C (room temperature), 4 °C, and -70 °C.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from environmental samples (wastewater, rivers) and clinical sources (urine, wounds, etc.).
- Adaptation/Tolerance: The phages showed significant stability at 4 °C and -70 °C, indicating good cold tolerance. The loss of activity at 37 °C over long periods suggests they are not adapted for long-term survival at body temperature.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater, rivers, and clinical samples (urine, wounds).

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophages were isolated from diverse sources, including environmental samples (wastewater, rivers) and clinical specimens (urine, wounds). Optimal lytic activity and phage amplification were consistently observed at 37 °C. Regarding thermal stability, the phages demonstrated excellent long-term survival when stored at 4 °C or frozen at -70 °C (with or without glycerol). Conversely, prolonged storage at 37 °C resulted in a complete loss of activity over six months. The phages were tested at room temperature (22–24 °C) and showed activity retention, suggesting mesophilic characteristics, but the data highlights a critical temperature dependency for long-term preservation, favoring refrigeration or freezing over ambient or body temperature storage.

Concise Summary:

Overall, the phages exhibited tolerance to a wide temperature spectrum, suggesting robustness for potential food biocontrol applications. The phages were sourced from municipal sewage and tested for stability across temperatures ranging from −20 °C to 80 °C.
