Paper: PMC13064462.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but assays were performed at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Phages retained titers after incubation at 4 °C, 37 °C, 50 °C, 60 °C, and 70 °C; PJNB030 and PJNB032 retained titers after 80 °C for 60 min.
- Incubation conditions: Assays were performed at 37 °C for various durations (e.g., 1 h for pH stability, 12 h for plaque assay, 72 h for biofilm assay).
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from a contaminated fermentation broth.
- Thermophilic adaptation: No explicit mention.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: No explicit mention.
- Mesophilic growth: Implied by optimal growth at 37 °C.
- Heat tolerance: Demonstrated by the retention of phage titers after exposure to 80 °C for 60 min for two of the three phages.
- Cold tolerance: Assays were performed at 4 °C.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: Not discussed.
- Membrane adaptations: Not discussed.
- Thermal stress responses: Demonstrated by phage survival at elevated temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/source: Contaminated fermentation broth.

Concise Summary:
Three phages were isolated from a contaminated fermentation broth. Phenotypic characterization showed that the phages maintained stability across a broad pH range (pH 4–10). Thermal stability assays indicated that while one phage was inactivated at 80 °C for 40 min, the other two phages retained measurable titers even after prolonged exposure to 80 °C (up to 60 min). The optimal growth and assay temperature was consistently 37 °C, with assays also conducted at 4 °C. The phages exhibited significant environmental resilience, suggesting robust stability beyond typical mesophilic conditions. The study utilized these thermal parameters to characterize the phages' environmental tolerance for industrial application.



Paper: PMC13125745.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37°C (for bacterial growth/phage propagation).
- Growth temperature range: 37°C (for bacterial growth).
- Survival temperature range: The phage retained infectivity over a temperature range of -20 to 50°C. The endolysin and its fusion protein retained activity over a temperature range of -20 to 60°C.
- Incubation conditions: Bacterial growth was typically performed with shaking at 37°C. Phage stability was tested at various temperatures.
- Environmental source/habitat: Sewage samples were used for phage isolation.
- Mention of adaptations: No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was given. The stability data indicates tolerance across a wide range, suggesting robustness, but no specific adaptation mechanism was detailed.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage samples were the source of the bacteriophage.

Concise Summary:
The bacterial host was cultured optimally at 37°C. The bacteriophage exhibited stability across a broad temperature range, retaining infectivity between -20°C and 50°C. The derived endolysin and its fluorescent fusion protein demonstrated even greater thermal stability, maintaining activity between -20°C and 60°C. The phage was isolated from sewage. The data supports the use of these components in various temperature regimes, indicating significant thermal robustness for downstream applications.
