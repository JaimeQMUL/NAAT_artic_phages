Paper: PMC13211797.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- No specific optimal, range, or survival temperatures were provided for the bacteria or their derived vesicles.
- No mention of thermophilic, psychrophilic, mesophilic, heat tolerance, cold tolerance, or temperature-adapted enzymes/membranes was found.

Environmental Context:
- The bacteria were studied in various contexts, including the gut, oral cavity, and systemic circulation, but no extreme environmental isolation was noted.

Concise Summary:
This review focuses on the role of bacterial extracellular vesicles (BEVs) in the gut-brain axis, neuroinflammation, and neurological diseases. The analysis details the composition, biodistribution, and mechanisms of action of BEVs derived from both pathogenic and commensal bacteria. Pathogenic BEVs (e.g., from *H. pylori*, *P. gingivalis*, *E. coli* O104:H4) are shown to induce neuroinflammation, disrupt the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and impair cognitive function via mechanisms involving TLR4/NLRP3 activation and the trigeminal nerve. Conversely, BEVs from probiotics (e.g., *Lactobacillus* species, *Bifidobacterium*) exhibit potent anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects, improving neurotransmission (GABAergic and glutamatergic) and cognitive function in models of AD, stress, and hepatic encephalopathy. The therapeutic potential lies in the BBB-crossing capability and modifiability of BEVs for drug delivery. The study emphasizes that BEVs act as key mediators in host-microbiota communication, linking gut dysbiosis to systemic and neurological pathology. No thermal adaptation data was available for the organisms discussed.
