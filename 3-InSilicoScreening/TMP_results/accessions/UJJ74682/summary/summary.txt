Paper: PMC12656726.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for initial screening, plaque assays, and general incubation).
- Growth temperature range: Tested across a wide range, including 4 °C to 80 °C for stability testing.
- Survival temperature range: Demonstrated remarkable stability at 65 °C, retaining infectivity for up to five hours. Infectious particles were still detectable after exposure to 80 °C for two hours.
- Incubation conditions: Standard incubation was performed at 37 °C. Extended stability tests were conducted at 65 °C and 70 °C.
- Thermophilic adaptation: Showed exceptional thermal stability, significantly exceeding the typical loss of viability observed for most bacteriophages at 60–65 °C.
- Heat tolerance: Possesses high heat tolerance, maintaining infectivity at temperatures where other reference phages were rapidly inactivated.
- Cold tolerance: Stability was tested down to 4 °C.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: The capsid structure was noted for its robustness, suggesting structural adaptation to thermal stress.
- Thermal stress responses: The phage exhibited first-order thermal inactivation kinetics at 70 °C, with an average reduction time of ~1.8 h.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolated from sewage samples.
- Extreme Environment Isolation: The phage was isolated from an environmental source (sewage) and demonstrated stability under conditions (e.g., 65 °C) that challenge typical mesophilic phages.
- Explicit Classification: The authors highlighted the 'extraordinary thermal stability' and 'high level of structural robustness' as key features, suggesting adaptation beyond typical mesophilic ranges for its capsid structure.

Paper: 42154063.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Enzyme activity was characterized across a broad temperature range of 4-90 ℃.
- Specific enzyme activities were measured at 50 ℃ for both xylanase and laccase.
- Optimal activity was observed at 50 ℃ for both key enzymes.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from high-altitude regions (Pangi-Chamba Himalayas).
- The isolates are described as extremophilic.

Concise Summary:
Lignocellulolytic bacteria were isolated from the high-altitude Pangi-Chamba Himalayas, suggesting adaptation to extreme environments. The characterized enzymes (laccase and xylanase) exhibit remarkable thermal stability, showing measurable activity across a broad range of 4-90 ℃. Optimal activity for both enzymes was determined at 50 ℃. The authors highlight the robustness of these high-altitude isolates for industrial applications, indicating potential for thermotolerance.
