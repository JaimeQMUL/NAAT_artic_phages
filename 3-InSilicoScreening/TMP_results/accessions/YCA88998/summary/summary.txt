Paper: PMC12428618.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal biofilm formation was observed at 37 °C (day 1), 25 °C (day 3), and 15 °C (day 5) for the most robust biofilm formation.
- Biofilm cell counts for all tested strains generally showed the highest counts at 37 °C after 1 day, followed by a gradual decrease to the lowest counts on day 7.
- The study suggests that biofilm formation is influenced by temperature and maturation period, with the highest biofilm cell counts observed at 25 °C for 3 days and 15 °C for 5 days across the most mature biofilms.
- UVC resistance was significantly lower for biofilms matured at 15 °C compared to those matured at 37 °C or 25 °C, indicating increased resistance at lower temperatures.
- Exopolysaccharide (EPS) content increased significantly as the temperature decreased, with the highest EPS levels observed at 15 °C.
- The study implies that low-temperature environments enhance biofilm resistance through increased EPS production.

Environmental Context:
- The organism was isolated from an occluded biliary stent, suggesting a clinical/internal body environment.
- The study tested growth conditions across a range including 15 °C, 25 °C, and 37 °C.

Concise Summary:
Biofilm formation and UVC resistance of the tested *E. coli* strains were assessed across maturation temperatures of 15 °C, 25 °C, and 37 °C. Biofilm formation was found to be highly dependent on both temperature and maturation time. While the highest biofilm cell counts were observed at 37 °C after 1 day, the most robust biofilms were noted at 25 °C (3 days) and 15 °C (5 days). A key finding is that biofilm resistance to UVC treatment and the production of exopolysaccharides (EPSs) both increased significantly as the maturation temperature decreased, with the lowest temperature (15 °C) correlating with the highest EPS content and greatest UVC resistance. The organism was isolated from a biliary stent, suggesting adaptation to a complex, potentially low-temperature, internal environment. The data strongly suggest that low-temperature maturation enhances biofilm resilience mechanisms, such as increased EPS production, in this species.
