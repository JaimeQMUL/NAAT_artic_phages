Paper: PMC9319786.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for bacterial growth and phage activity).
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but optimal activity observed at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Stable at 4 °C and -70 °C (with or without glycerol) over six months. Activity was completely lost at 37 °C after 6 months of storage, suggesting high sensitivity to prolonged incubation at body temperature.
- Incubation conditions: Phage activity was maintained in fresh urine samples (pH = 6) after incubation for up to 24 hours at 37 °C.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from environmental samples (wastewater, rivers) and clinical samples (UTI, wounds).
- Thermal adaptation: No mention of thermophilic or psychrophilic adaptation. The phages exhibit mesophilic characteristics, optimized for human body temperature (37 °C) for lytic activity, but show significant loss of activity over extended periods at this temperature.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater, rivers, and clinical sites (UTI, wounds).

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophages were characterized using bacterial hosts that are typically mesophilic pathogens, with optimal lytic activity observed at 37 °C. The phages demonstrated excellent long-term stability when stored at 4 °C or frozen at -70 °C, retaining activity over six months. Conversely, prolonged storage at 37 °C resulted in a complete loss of activity. Furthermore, the phages maintained activity when incubated in a simulated infection site (urine sample) at 37 °C for up to 24 hours. The phages were isolated from both environmental sources (wastewater, rivers) and clinical infections, confirming their relevance in the context of human-associated pathogens.

Paper: PMC13118744.1.txt

- The optimal decolorization temperature is related to the optimal temperature of degradation enzymes, such as azo reductase.
- The organism exhibited higher decolorization activity between 30 and 45 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from soil samples.
- The authors did not explicitly classify the organism regarding temperature adaptation (e.g., mesophile, thermophile, psychrophile), but the optimal activity was found at 45 °C, suggesting mesophilic or moderate thermotolerance within the tested range.

Concise Summary:
The organism was isolated from soil and demonstrated optimal decolorization activity for methyl red at 45 °C. Its decolorization efficiency was highest within a temperature range of 30–45 °C, suggesting mesophilic characteristics. Activity significantly dropped at 50 °C. The optimal temperature is linked to the activity of the degradation enzymes, such as azo reductase. The organism was not explicitly classified by the authors regarding thermal adaptation, but its performance profile suggests robust activity in the moderate temperature range of 30–45 °C.
