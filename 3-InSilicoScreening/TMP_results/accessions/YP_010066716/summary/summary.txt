Paper: PMC10217414.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Storage at 4 °C resulted in pathogen reductions of ca. 2.2 to 3.1 log CFU/slice over 90 days.
- Storage at 20 °C resulted in pathogen reductions of ca. 2.2 to 3.1 log CFU/slice over 90 days.
- Initial levels of all three pathogens were reduced from ca. 4.6 log CFU/slice to ca. 1.3 to 1.7 log CFU/slice within 28 days during storage at 20 °C.
- No significant differences in pathogen levels were observed after 90 days of storage at 4 °C or 20 °C.

Environmental Context:
- The pathogens were studied on slices of an all-beef soppressata, a dry-cured, fermented meat product.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the fate of three foodborne pathogens on beef soppressata slices stored at 4 °C and 20 °C over 90 days. Pathogen levels decreased by 2.2 to 3.3 log CFU/slice in both storage conditions. The data indicate that the product matrix itself did not provide a favorable environment for the survival or outgrowth of these pathogens during refrigerated storage. No specific information regarding optimal growth temperature, survival temperature range, or explicit classification (e.g., psychrophilic, mesophilic) was provided for the pathogens in relation to the storage temperatures tested, other than the observed reduction rates at 4 °C and 20 °C.
