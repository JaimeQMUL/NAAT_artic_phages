Concise Summary:
This study focuses on the genomic characterization of putative plasmid prophages found in species belonging to the *Bacillus cereus* group. The analysis is based on comparative genomics of plasmid DNA recovered from various isolates. The research details the identification of multiple groups of putative prophages, characterized by shared structural and replication genes (e.g., tail components, partitioning systems, recombinases). The findings emphasize the high diversity and potential underestimation of phage populations in bacterial whole-genome sequencing data. The methodology relies heavily on bioinformatics tools (HMM profiling, pangenome analysis) to classify these elements into distinct groups (e.g., Group 1, Group 2, etc.). The authors stress that experimental confirmation, such as stress induction (e.g., with mitomycin C) or Southern blotting, is necessary to confirm the functional status of these prophages, distinguishing between active, degenerated, or integrated forms. The study concludes that the identified elements represent a significant reservoir of novel phage genetic material, suggesting the need for continued large-scale genomic surveillance.
