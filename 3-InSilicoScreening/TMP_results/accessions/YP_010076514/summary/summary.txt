Paper: PMC13153029.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Stable from 4 °C to 50 °C.
- Active at 37 °C (physiological temperature).
- Stability declined after 80 min at 60 °C, with an 87.8% reduction in titer.
- Rapid decline observed at 70 °C.
- Stable after 1 year of storage at 4 °C.
- Optimal activity observed between room temperature and human physiological temperature (20 °C–37 °C).

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from untreated sewage at a hospital.
- No explicit classification regarding thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic adaptation was provided; the stability profile suggests mesophilic characteristics with good cold tolerance (stable at 4 °C) and moderate heat tolerance up to 50 °C.

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibits robust stability across a wide temperature range, maintaining activity from 4 °C up to 50 °C. It is optimal at physiological temperatures (20 °C–37 °C) and demonstrated long-term stability when stored at 4 °C for over a year. Thermal stability testing showed a significant decline in titer after 80 minutes at 60 °C and rapid loss of activity at 70 °C. The phage was isolated from untreated sewage, suggesting an environmental origin. The stability profile indicates mesophilic characteristics with notable cold tolerance and moderate heat tolerance, but no specific classification (e.g., thermophilic) was assigned by the authors.
