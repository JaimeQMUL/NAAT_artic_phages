Paper: PMC13208690.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 30 °C (under NTNL conditions).
- Growth temperature range: The organism showed robust growth at 30 °C (NTNL) and also at 40 °C (HLHT).
- Survival temperature range: The wild-type struggled to survive at 42 °C, while the mutant exhibited accelerated growth at 42 °C, 2000 μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.
- Incubation conditions: Cultures were maintained at 30 °C (NTNL) and tested under high-light/high-temperature (HLHT) conditions (e.g., 40 °C, 2000 μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹).
- Environmental source/habitat: Not explicitly stated, but the study models conditions relevant to environmental stress (HLHT).
- Thermophilic adaptation: The mutant showed enhanced growth and survival under elevated temperatures (40–42 °C) compared to the wild-type.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: No specific data provided.
- Mesophilic growth: Growth was observed at 30 °C, consistent with mesophilic conditions.
- Heat tolerance: The C252Y mutation significantly enhanced tolerance to high-temperature stress combined with high light.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: The study analyzed the expression of heat shock proteins (HSPs) and sigma factors (e.g., SigC, HrcA) in response to thermal stress.
- Membrane adaptations: Not explicitly detailed.
- Thermal stress responses: The mutation enhanced stress resilience, leading to improved photosynthetic efficiency and reduced photoinhibition under HLHT.

Environmental Context:
- The organism was studied under controlled laboratory conditions simulating high-light and high-temperature stress, rather than being isolated from an extreme natural environment.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the physiological and transcriptomic effects of a specific mutation (F1-α-C252Y) in the ATP synthase subunit of a cyanobacterium under high-light–high-temperature (HLHT) stress. The organism exhibits mesophilic growth at 30 °C (NTNL). The mutant strain demonstrated significantly enhanced tolerance to HLHT conditions (e.g., 40–42 °C) compared to the wild-type, which struggled at these elevated temperatures. This enhanced thermal and light stress resilience is attributed to the mutation, which leads to increased intracellular ATP synthase complex levels and overall ATP synthesis activity. Metabolically, the mutation triggers a widespread transcriptional remodeling, upregulating genes in the electron transport chain (e.g., *petH*, *petA*, *petD*), the Calvin-Benson-Bassham (CBB) cycle (e.g., *fbp*, *rpiA*, *rsb*), and glycogen synthesis pathways. Furthermore, the mutant shows altered regulation of stress response genes, including the upregulation of the heat-inducible repressor (*hrcA*) and the downregulation of certain heat shock proteins, suggesting an optimized mechanism for coping with thermal stress. The overall findings indicate that the mutation boosts energy production and metabolic efficiency, conferring superior survival and growth rates under challenging, elevated temperature and light regimes.
