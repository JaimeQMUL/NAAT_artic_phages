Paper: PMC13208690.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 30 °C (under NTNL conditions).
- Growth temperature range: The study utilized conditions ranging from 30 °C (NTNL) up to 42 °C (HLHT testing).
- Survival temperature range: The wild-type strain struggled to survive at 42 °C, while the mutant exhibited accelerated growth at 42 °C.
- Incubation conditions: Cultures were grown in liquid BG11 medium in a bioreactor under controlled conditions, including NTNL (30 °C, 280 μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹), and various HLHT conditions (e.g., 40 °C, 2000 μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹).
- Heat tolerance: The mutant strain showed significantly enhanced tolerance to high-light–high-temperature (HLHT) stress compared to the wild-type strain, which struggled under these conditions.
- Thermal stress responses: The mutation induced changes in gene expression related to heat shock proteins (HSPs) and sigma factors, suggesting a mechanism for thermal stress adaptation. The upregulation of the heat-inducible transcriptional repressor (HrcA) was noted.

Environmental Context:
- The organism was studied under controlled laboratory conditions simulating environmental stress (HLHT).
- No explicit classification (e.g., mesophile, thermophile) was provided by the authors, but the context implies adaptation to fluctuating, stressful environments.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the physiological and transcriptomic responses of a cyanobacterium to high-light–high-temperature (HLHT) stress. The organism demonstrated superior growth and survival under HLHT conditions when carrying a specific mutation in the ATP synthase subunit $\alpha$ (C252Y) compared to the wild-type strain, which struggled at elevated temperatures (e.g., 42 °C). The optimal growth temperature observed under normal light and temperature (NTNL) was 30 °C. The enhanced tolerance to HLHT stress in the mutant was linked to increased intracellular ATP synthase complex levels, improved photosynthetic efficiency (higher Fv/Fm and ETR), and widespread metabolic remodeling. Transcriptomic analysis revealed significant changes in the expression of genes related to the electron transport chain, Calvin-Benson-Bassham (CBB) cycle, and glycogen synthesis, all contributing to enhanced stress resilience. The mutation also modulated the expression of stress-related genes, including the upregulation of the heat-inducible transcriptional repressor (HrcA) and the downregulation of certain heat shock proteins, indicating a robust thermal stress response mechanism. The overall findings suggest that the mutation facilitates adaptation to challenging, elevated temperature and light regimes.
