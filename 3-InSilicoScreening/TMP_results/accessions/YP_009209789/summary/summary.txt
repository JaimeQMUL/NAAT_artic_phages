- The organisms were sourced from various natural and agricultural contexts, but no specific extreme environmental isolation (e.g., hydrothermal vents, deep sea) was detailed for the entire group.

Concise Summary:
The paper provides a comparative analysis of seven types of phosphate transporters across 40 species spanning prokaryotes, fungi, algae, and diverse plant lineages. The focus is on the phylogenetic relationships, structural motifs, and transcriptomic expression patterns of these transporters in response to phosphate availability and tissue type. The analysis reveals that the seven transporter types have distinct evolutionary origins, with PHT3s, pPTs, and SPX-domain PTs being of eukaryotic origin, while PHT1s, PHT2s, PHT4s, and G3Pps show prokaryotic origins. Key findings include the differential expression patterns of these transporters, particularly the induction of PHT1s and G3Pps under phosphate starvation, and the specialized roles of transporters in root-to-shoot translocation (e.g., PHO1). The study concludes by proposing that PHT1s, G3Pps, and SPX-domain PTs are promising candidates for genetic engineering strategies aimed at developing phosphate-efficient crops. No thermal physiology data was presented.

Concise Summary:
This review focuses on the molecular and physiological mechanisms by which microbial symbionts modulate insect resistance to insecticides. The discussion centers on three core resistance mechanisms: reduced penetration, enhanced metabolism (oxidative and hydrolytic detoxification), and target-site modification. Symbiont involvement is described through direct enzymatic degradation of insecticides (e.g., by bacterial enzymes) or by reprogramming host detoxification pathways (e.g., upregulating P450s, GSTs, and CarEs). The evidence is highly mechanistic, detailing molecular interactions, gene expression changes, and metabolic fluxes, but contains no data regarding the thermal physiology, growth temperatures, or environmental temperature adaptations of the involved microbes or insects.
