Concise Summary:
The study analyzed the environmental DNA (eDNA) of various pathogen markers (including bacteria and parasites) recovered from surface sponges and fecal samples in natural areas. While no specific thermal characteristics of the detected microbes were reported, the research established a strong correlation between eDNA marker diversity and environmental climate variables. Specifically, pathogen marker diversity positively correlated with precipitation and negatively correlated with multiple temperature metrics (annual mean, maximum warm month, mean warm quarter, and mean cold quarter temperatures). The authors interpreted this as evidence that environmental conditions, particularly cooler and more humid environments, favor the persistence of microbial DNA in the environment, suggesting that eDNA detection reflects DNA persistence rather than viable organism load. The findings suggest that climatic factors are the primary drivers influencing the detection of pathogen-related markers at large geographic scales.
