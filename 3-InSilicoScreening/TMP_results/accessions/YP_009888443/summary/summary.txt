- Biofilm formation was found to be significantly enhanced at 4 °C, particularly in the presence of a specific medium.
- Biofilm formation was generally observed to be more pronounced at higher temperatures (23 °C and 37 °C) compared to 14 °C.

- The study compared biofilm formation across different temperatures, suggesting temperature is a significant influencing factor.

Environmental Context:
- The isolates were sourced from chicken feces and chicken appendix, indicating an animal/foodborne origin.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated biofilm formation and essential oil efficacy against the pathogen across a range of temperatures, specifically 4 °C, 14 °C, 23 °C, and 37 °C. Biofilm formation was found to be significantly influenced by temperature, with enhanced formation observed at 4 °C, 23 °C, and 37 °C, and the highest overall potential noted at 37 °C. Biofilm degradation efficacy of tested essential oils was most pronounced at 14 °C and 37 °C, while 23 °C showed lower degradation rates. The pathogen was isolated from chicken sources, suggesting an environmental/foodborne context. The data supports temperature as a critical factor influencing both biofilm establishment and the antimicrobial/biofilm-disrupting potential of essential oils.
