Paper: PMC13133039.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

- No mention of thermophilic, psychrophilic, mesophilic, heat tolerance, cold tolerance, or temperature-adapted enzymes/membranes.

Environmental Context:
- The organisms were isolated from root nodules of compatible legume plants, soil, and were associated with plant-microbe interactions.

Concise Summary:
This study focuses on the diversity and evolution of quorum-sensing systems in a genus of bacteria associated with legume root nodules. The analysis utilized comparative genomics across 142 complete genomes. The research details the genetic distribution of AHL-based QS systems (LuxI/LuxR) across chromosomes and plasmids, highlighting the role of horizontal gene transfer in shaping QS evolution. Key findings include the identification of multiple, independent acquisition events of QS systems on the chromosome, and the observation that plasmid-encoded systems exhibit greater diversity and mobility compared to chromosomal ones. The study also analyzed the coevolutionary patterns, noting that chromosomal pairs show evidence of stable, vertical inheritance, while plasmid-borne systems show high plasticity. The research also identified evidence of QSS gene exchange between plasmids and chromosomes, and documented instances of QSS degradation (pseudogenes or truncated genes). The environmental context is consistently linked to plant-microbe interactions, particularly nitrogen fixation and symbiosis with legumes.
