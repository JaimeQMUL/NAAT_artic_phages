Concise Summary:
This review focuses on the genomic, structural, and evolutionary characteristics of jumbo bacteriophages. The phages were isolated from a wide variety of environmental sources, including marine sediments, water, soil, composts, animal feces, silkworms, and plant tissues. The paper details the large genome size (200–500 kbp), complex structures, and unique genetic content, such as the presence of tRNA and chaperonin genes, which suggest an increased independence from host molecular machinery. Key physiological aspects discussed include the phage's ability to regulate gene expression using phage-encoded RNA polymerases, the development of proteinaceous nucleus-like compartments for DNA protection, and mechanisms to evade host defenses like CRISPR-Cas systems. The evolutionary discussion suggests that jumbo phages may have evolved from smaller phages through horizontal gene transfer. No specific thermal adaptation data was provided for downstream classification.
