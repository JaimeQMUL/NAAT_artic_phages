Environmental Context:
- The samples were collected from freshwater aquaculture environments, specifically water, sediment, and intestinal samples associated with three types of aquaculture ponds (grass carp, crayfish, and crab ponds).

Concise Summary:
This study characterized the resistome and mobilome across different freshwater aquaculture modes using metagenomic sequencing. The analysis focused on the distribution and co-enrichment of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) and mobile genetic elements (MGEs) in gut, sediment, and water samples. The intestinal microbiome was identified as a critical hotspot for resistance accumulation, particularly in one specific aquaculture system. High-risk ARGs were found to be significantly more abundant in the gut compared to other compartments. Furthermore, the study noted the co-localization of ARGs, MGEs, and virulence factor genes within prophage sequences, suggesting phage-mediated co-dissemination of resistance and virulence traits. The analysis provided a basis for assessing ARG risks within the One Health framework, but contained no physiological data related to temperature adaptation or thermal ecology.

Concise Summary:
This study focuses on the metabolic profiling and drug target identification in a carbapenem-resistant strain of *Klebsiella pneumoniae* using genome-scale metabolic modeling. The analysis integrates multi-omics data (genomic, transcriptomic) to prioritize metabolic pathways for novel antimicrobial development. The research details the manual refinement of the metabolic network, the identification of topological choke-points, and the scoring of pathways based on druggability, essentiality, and upregulation during carbapenem exposure. Key target pathways identified include those involved in LPS biosynthesis, preQ0 biosynthesis, lipoylation, methionine salvage, flavin biosynthesis, and energy metabolism (TCA cycle). The metabolic analysis is highly focused on biochemical function and drug potential rather than physiological adaptation to temperature extremes.

- Cold tolerance: Stable at 4 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Hospital sewage water sample.

Concise Summary:
The phage was isolated from hospital sewage water and was characterized using standard laboratory conditions, with bacterial growth and phage assays optimally performed at 37 °C. The phage exhibits good stability at 4 °C, retaining its titer, while its activity significantly decreases at higher temperatures, showing a loss of titer at 60 °C and 80 °C compared to 4 °C. The phage is classified as mesophilic, thriving under conditions suitable for human/animal pathogens, and demonstrated long-term stability when stored at 4 °C. No evidence of thermophilic adaptation was noted; rather, it shows limited heat tolerance above 50 °C.
