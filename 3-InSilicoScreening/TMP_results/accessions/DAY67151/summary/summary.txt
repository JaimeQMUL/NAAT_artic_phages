Paper: PMC13198863.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Moderate cold and heat exposure during early pregnancy were associated with a smaller crown–rump length (CRL) at 12 weeks.
- Specific associations noted: Exposure to 19.2°C during weeks 1–6 was associated with a 7.2 mm smaller CRL (vs 9.0°C reference).
- Cold exposure: Exposure to 3.6°C during weeks 1–11 was associated with a 7.6 mm smaller CRL (vs 9.0°C reference).
- The effects were most evident at 12 weeks of gestation, but not at 8 or 10 weeks.
- The study suggests early gestational development is sensitive to ambient temperature.

Environmental Context:
- Ambient air temperature during early pregnancy (first trimester).

Concise Summary:
Ambient temperature exposure during early pregnancy was linked to reduced embryonic and early fetal size, measured by a smaller crown–rump length (CRL) at 12 weeks gestation. Both moderate heat (e.g., 19.2°C) and moderate cold (e.g., 3.6°C) exposures were associated with smaller CRLs compared to a reference temperature (9.0°C). The sensitivity of early development to ambient temperature was observed during the first trimester, with the effects being most pronounced at 12 weeks. The study does not discuss microbial physiology, adaptation, or enzyme activity, as the subject is human fetal development influenced by ambient environmental temperature.
