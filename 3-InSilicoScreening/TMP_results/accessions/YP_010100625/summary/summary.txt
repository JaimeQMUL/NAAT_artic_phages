Paper: PMC13070987.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

- Thermophilic/Psychrophilic/Mesophilic: The phage demonstrated stability at 4 °C and optimal activity at 37 °C, consistent with mesophilic conditions.
- Heat tolerance: Stable at 4 °C; showed significant loss of titer at 60 °C and 80 °C.
- Cold tolerance: Stable at 4 °C.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: No specific mention of temperature-adapted enzymes.
- Membrane adaptations: No mention of membrane adaptations.
- Thermal stress responses: Stability was tested across a range of temperatures (4 °C to 80 °C).

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Hospital sewage water sample.

Concise Summary:
The phage was isolated from hospital sewage water and was characterized using standard laboratory conditions, with optimal growth and activity observed at 37 °C. The phage exhibited good cold tolerance, remaining stable at 4 °C, and was found to be stable for long-term storage at this temperature. Thermal stability testing showed that while the phage maintained its titer at 4 °C, its infectivity significantly decreased at higher temperatures, losing titer below detection levels at 60 °C and 80 °C. The overall profile suggests mesophilic adaptation, with robust performance at standard physiological temperatures (37 °C) and good cold stability, but limited tolerance to elevated temperatures.

Paper: PMC13188874.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Incubation temperatures tested were 12°C and 32°C.
- For bacterial persistence, significant differences in recovery were observed between 12°C and 32°C after 8 months, with higher recovery at 12°C.
- For protozoan persistence (mRNA detection), significantly fewer oocysts were detected at 32°C compared to 12°C throughout the experiment.
- For protozoan persistence (mRNA detection), the greatest difference between temperatures was seen after 5 months, with significantly more oocysts at 12°C than 32°C.
- For bacterial persistence, the differences between temperatures were greatest after 9 months, with higher recovery at 12°C.
- For protozoan persistence (mRNA detection), the temperature impact was noted almost immediately, with far fewer oocysts detected at the higher temperature.

Environmental Context:
- The study simulated environmental conditions using soil inoculated with plant tissue, incubated at 12°C and 32°C.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the persistence of a bacterium and two protozoan parasites in soil following simulated tilling under, comparing incubation at 12°C and 32°C. The bacterium showed significantly higher recovery at 12°C compared to 32°C after 8 months. For the protozoa, mRNA detection indicated a temperature-dependent decline, with significantly lower levels detected at 32°C compared to 12°C, suggesting higher temperature sensitivity for the molecular markers. The experiment did not test for thermophilic or psychrophilic adaptation, but rather assessed survival and molecular persistence across a range including ambient low (12°C) and high (32°C) temperatures, suggesting mesophilic conditions were relevant to the observed decay patterns.
