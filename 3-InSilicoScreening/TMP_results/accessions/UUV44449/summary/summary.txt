Paper: PMC11946847.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal pH values for phage Lydia ranged from 7 to 10.
- Phage stability was assessed across a range of temperatures: −20 °C, +4 °C, +28 °C, +37 °C, +50 °C, and +60 °C for 1 h.
- Titer remained relatively stable between −20 °C and +60 °C.
- Complete loss of phage viability was observed after 1 h incubation at +70 °C.
- The phage exhibited rapid adsorption at +37 °C.
- The one-step growth experiment was conducted at +37 °C.
- The phage was found to be stable at +4 °C, suggesting cold tolerance.
- The phage was tested at elevated temperatures, with stability assessed up to +60 °C.

Environmental Context:
- The phage was isolated from a river water sample (Selenga River).
- The phage was found to infect *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, a bacterium found in diverse environments including water, soil, and human mucous membranes.

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibits optimal pH stability between 7 and 10. Its stability profile shows relative resilience across a broad temperature range, maintaining infectivity after incubation at −20 °C up to +60 °C. However, viability is completely lost after 1 hour at +70 °C. The phage was characterized in vitro using conditions mimicking natural environments, including isolation from river water and infection of *P. aeruginosa* strains. The experimental data supports the phage's ability to function and maintain stability across mesophilic conditions (optimal at 37 °C) and demonstrates cold tolerance down to −20 °C, while showing limited thermal tolerance above 60 °C.
