Paper: 42175751.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- High temperatures are associated with increased risk of adverse mental health outcomes.
- A marked nonlinear increase in risk was observed, reaching relative risk values close to two at temperatures exceeding 40°C.

Environmental Context:
- The study analyzed hospitalizations in major Italian cities during the summer months (June-September).

Concise Summary:
The analysis established a strong, nonlinear association between elevated ambient temperatures (specifically exceeding 40°C) and increased rates of mental health hospitalizations. The modeling framework utilized a Distributed Lag Nonlinear Model (DLNM) within a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) to assess this heat-related impact across multiple Italian cities during the summer season. The primary finding is the significant increase in risk associated with extreme heat exposure, suggesting a physiological stressor related to high ambient temperatures, although the study does not pertain to microbial growth parameters.
