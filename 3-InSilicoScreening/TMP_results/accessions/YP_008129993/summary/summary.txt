Concise Summary:
The research investigates the biogeochemical processes driving subsurface oxygen maxima (SOMs) in the oligotrophic Sargasso Sea. The study utilizes high-resolution metatranscriptomics, viromics, and historical oceanographic data to link enhanced viral lysis (the 'viral shunt') to increased nutrient recycling and subsequent oxygen accumulation. The microbial community structure and function are analyzed across different water depths (surface, base of mixed layer, SOM, deep chlorophyll maximum). Key findings include elevated cyanophage and total cyanophage infection rates within the SOM, suggesting active viral replication. This viral activity is hypothesized to stimulate heterotrophic bacterial catabolism of dissolved organic matter (DOM), which releases ammonium, thereby fueling primary production by cyanobacteria. The overall process describes a microbial loop where viral lysis drives nutrient cycling, leading to oxygen supersaturation in the SOM. The study emphasizes the biogeochemical impact of viral dynamics in the open ocean, with no explicit thermal physiological data provided for classification.
