Thermal Evidence:
- Survival temperature range: 40 to 90 °C.
- Some phages can resist pasteurization temperatures.
- One phage was noted to be thermostable.
- One phage was found to express its best lytic action at 37 °C.
- One phage was found to be stable at a wide pH range (between 4 and 9) but inactivated if subjected to a temperature higher than 60 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Phages were found in various environments, including food, mucosal surfaces, and hostile environments like thermal springs of volcanic origin.

Concise Summary:
Bacteriophages exhibit a broad survival temperature range, generally between 40 and 90 °C, with some strains demonstrating resistance to pasteurization temperatures. Specific lytic activity was observed optimally at 37 °C. Stability profiles vary significantly; for instance, one phage was noted to be stable across a wide pH range (4–9) but susceptible to temperatures exceeding 60 °C. The phages are naturally abundant in diverse sources, including human and animal mucosal surfaces, food, and geothermal/volcanic thermal springs. The literature does not explicitly classify the phages based on thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic adaptation, but the observed survival ranges suggest adaptation to mesophilic conditions, with some notable thermostability.

Paper: PMC13209213.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for Caco-2 cell culture).
- Growth temperature range: 30 °C (for general culture/plate incubation) and 37 °C (for human cell culture).
- Survival temperature range: Psychotropic ability noted, with survival tested at 4 °C (in culture broth and on meat) and 12 °C (for motility and adhesion).
- Incubation conditions: Assays were performed at 4 °C, 12 °C, and 37 °C.
- Cold tolerance: The organism is described as psychotropic, capable of multiplying in foods stored under refrigeration (4 °C).
- Thermal stress responses: Cold acclimation involves the transient induction followed by the effective repression of cold shock protein (Csp) genes.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolated from animal reservoirs (porcine and bovine) and tested in food matrices (ham, beef steak) simulating food chain passage.
- Extreme Environment: Not explicitly stated as isolated from an extreme environment.
- Explicit Classification: Described as psychotropic bacteria.

Concise Summary:
The organism exhibits psychotropic characteristics, allowing multiplication in food matrices stored under refrigeration (4 °C). Optimal growth for human cell culture was observed at 37 °C, while general culture and plate incubation were conducted at 30 °C. Survival and motility were specifically tested at 4 °C and 12 °C, demonstrating adaptation to cold-chain conditions. The study noted that cold acclimation involves transcriptional changes, including the repression of cold shock protein genes. The organism's ability to survive and multiply at low temperatures (4 °C) is a key feature relevant to food safety concerns.
