Avian leukosis virus subgroup J (ALV-J), Reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV), and Chicken infectious anemia virus (CIAV) are important breeder-derived pathogens (BDPs) that continue to threaten poultry health and productivity. These viruses are frequently associated with immunosuppression and coinfections, which complicate eradication efforts, particularly in indigenous chicken breeds. In this study, a TaqMan-based multiplex qPCR (M-qPCR) assay was developed for the simultaneous detection of ALV-J, REV, and CIAV. The assay exhibited excellent linearity (R2 > 0.99), high repeatability (intra-/inter-assay coefficient of variation < 5%), strong specificity with no cross-reactivity against common non-target avian pathogens, and high sensitivity with a detection limit of 10 copies/μL. Moreover, it reliably identified mixed infections even under conditions of unbalanced target concentrations. Parallel testing with commercial singleplex qPCR kits further demonstrated its reliability, yielding concordance rates of 95.8% for ALV-J, 98.3% for REV, and 100% for CIAV. Application of the assay to three local chicken breeds in Guizhou revealed positivity rates of 33.9% (40/118) for ALV-J, 9.3% (11/118) for REV, and 7.6% (9/118) for CIAV, with 20.4% mixed (double and triple) out of all positives. Moreover, serum samples proved more suitable than cloacal swabs for virus detection using the developed M-qPCR assay. In conclusion, the developed M-qPCR assay provides a reliable and efficient tool for surveillance of infections and coinfections in poultry flocks, supporting the prevention and control of BDPs.

Introduction

Breeder-derived pathogens (BDPs) in chickens, also known as vertically transmitted or egg-transmitted pathogens, are those passed from breeder hens to their offspring through the ovary or oviduct during embryonic development (Liao et al., 2022; Fandino et al., 2023). These pathogens can persist across generations and often spread horizontally within flocks, making them difficult to manage (Mugunthan et al., 2023; Song et al., 2024). BDPs are commonly linked to immunosuppression, which increases the risk of secondary infections and leads to poor growth, lower productivity, and higher mortality (Li et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022). Due to their transmission pattern and economic impact, eradication at the breeder level is considered the most effective and cost-efficient strategy (Kreager, 1998; Borodin et al., 2022).

Recent reports indicate the widespread presence of BDPs in China’s poultry industry (Ma et al., 2024). Unlike commercial broilers and layers raised under intensive management, indigenous chicken breeds are often reared in small-scale or free-range systems, which commonly lack effective biosecurity, standardized breeding, and veterinary oversight. These limitations, along with the absence of purification programs and mixed breeding sources, pose significant challenges to the eradication of BDPs in local breeds (Conan et al., 2012; Mujyambere et al., 2022; Lei et al., 2024).

Common BDPs in chickens include Salmonella pullorum (S. pullorum), Mycoplasma gallisepticum (MG), Avian leukosis virus (ALV), Reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV), Chicken infectious anemia virus (CIAV), Egg drop syndrome virus (EDSV), and Avian encephalomyelitis virus (AEV) (Welchman et al., 2009; Mcilwaine et al., 2021; Schat et al., 2021; Yadav et al., 2022). These pathogens can cause acute clinical signs such as anemia and tumors, as well as subclinical effects including reduced egg production, poor hatchability, and decreased chick quality, posing serious threats to the sustainable development of the poultry industry (Kannaki et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022; Shosha et al., 2024).

Subgroup J of ALV (ALV-J) has become a major target for surveillance because of its rapid spread, wide distribution, vertical transmission potential, and lack of effective control measures (Li et al., 2019). Recent studies revealed that REV and CIAV share similar transmission patterns with ALV-J and are frequently detected together in co-infected breeder flocks. Conventional single-target assays, such as virus isolation, ELISA, and standard PCR, can detect only one pathogen at a time, making it difficult to obtain a timely and comprehensive understanding of infection dynamics. Therefore, establishing a reliable multi-pathogen detection system is essential for improving breeder flock monitoring and reducing transmission risks.

Multiplex quantitative real-time PCR (M-qPCR) using TaqMan probe technology allows multiple pathogens to be detected in a single reaction by assigning distinct fluorescent channels to each target (Sun et al., 2023). Owing to its high accuracy, speed, and automation, M-qPCR has been widely applied in multi-pathogen surveillance (Wei et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2025). For example, Li et al. established a multiplex RT-qPCR assay capable of detecting six vertically transmitted avian viruses, which was successfully applied in clinical investigations of infection and coinfection patterns in poultry flocks, demonstrating the value of M-qPCR for rapid and accurate pathogen monitoring (Li et al., 2020).

A well-designed M-qPCR assay depends largely on the selection of primers and probes that target conserved sequences (Jeong et al., 2022). Among the major BDPs—ALV-J, REV, and CIAV—the gp85 gene of ALV-J determines subgroup specificity and mediates receptor binding, making it a critical molecular diagnostic target (Qu et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022). The gp90 gene of REV, encoding the major immunogenic envelope protein, is highly conserved and suitable for specific detection (Xue et al., 2012). The VP3 gene of CIAV, which induces apoptosis and exhibits strong sequence conservation across strains, serves as an ideal target for sensitive molecular assays (Sreekala et al., 2024). These conserved regions provide reliable targets for the design of primers and probes, ensuring the assay’s specificity and diagnostic performance.

In this study, we aim to develop a rapid, sensitive, and efficient M-qPCR assay for ALV-J, REV, and CIAV, three major BDPs. Specific primers and probes will be designed based on conserved genomic regions, and the assay will be optimized and preliminarily applied to indigenous chicken breeds in Guizhou Province. This method is expected to improve pathogen detection and provide technical support for the eradication of BDPs and conservation of local poultry genetic resources.

Nucleic acids of viral and bacterial strains

Nucleic acids used for plasmid construction

The ALV-J isolate GZ2024GY04J (GenBank accession no. PV239795) was isolated and preserved in our laboratory (College of Animal Science, Guizhou University). The target gene fragments of REV (GenBank accession no. PP526161) and CIAV (GenBank accession no. NC_001427) were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Primer and probe design

Three candidate primer–probe sets were designed using Beacon Designer 8, and their specificity was evaluated using Primer-BLAST. Further analysis of Tm values, GC content, and potential secondary structures was performed using Oligo 7. Based on the combined evaluation results, the most specific and optimally designed primer–probe set was synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Preparation of recombinant plasmids as positive standards

Construction of recombinant plasmids

To obtain stable target gene templates for downstream analysis, recombinant plasmids were constructed and used as positive standards in this study. The target gene fragments of ALV-J, REV, and CIAV were amplified by PCR, confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis, and purified using the Fast Pure Gel DNA Extraction Mini Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The purified amplicons were ligated into the pMD19-T vector (Takara, Beijing, China), followed by transformation into E. coli DH5α (Sangon, Shanghai, China) competent cells. Positive clones were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The recombinant plasmids were extracted using the EasyPure Plasmid MiniPrep Kit (TransGen, Beijing, China), and plasmid concentrations were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA).

Preparation of standard dilutions

The positive recombinant plasmids of the three viruses were diluted from their initial concentrations, based on calculated copy numbers, using double distilled water (ddH2O). Each was adjusted to 1 × 1010 copies/μL, followed by 10-fold serial dilutions down to 1 copy/μL. Dilutions at different concentrations were aliquoted and stored at −20 ℃ as positive standards. The plasmid copy number, equivalent to the viral genome copy number, was calculated using the following formula: Copies/μL = (6.02 × 1023) × (plasmid concentration in g/μL) / (plasmid length in bp × 660).

Establishment of a M-qPCR assay for the detection of ALV-J, REV, and CIAV

S-qPCR system establishment

Singleplex qPCR (S-qPCR) systems were first established for ALV-J, REV, and CIAV. Each 25 μL reaction contained 12.5 μL of Premix Ex Taq (Probe qPCR) (2×) (Takara), each primer (10 μM) and probe (10 μM) at four test volumes (0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 μL), 2 μL of the corresponding positive standard, and ddH2O to complete the volume. A total of 16 reaction combinations were tested, and the optimal concentrations were selected based on the lowest Ct value.

Amplification was performed using a two-step protocol: 95 ℃ for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 5 s and annealing/extension. Eight protocols with annealing/extension temperatures ranging from 56.0 ℃ to 64.0 ℃ were tested, and the one yielding the lowest Ct value was selected as optimal. Reactions were run on a CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, USA).

M-qPCR system establishment

To establish the M-qPCR system, equal volumes of each probe (100 μM) were combined and diluted with ddH2O (7/3 of the total volume) to yield a 10 μM mixed probe. Using the same approach, mixed forward primers, mixed reverse primers, and a mixed positive standard were obtained. These were used to develop the M-qPCR system, and the final reaction system and cycling conditions were optimized as described above.

Standard curve and performance evaluation

Based on the finalized M-qPCR reaction system and cycling conditions, the mixed positive standard at 1 × 109 copies/μL was serially diluted 10-fold using ddH2O to a final dilution of 1 × 10° copies/μL. Six concentrations ranging from 1 × 103 to 1 × 108 copies/μL were used as templates to construct the standard curve, with each concentration tested in triplicate. For specificity evaluation, the assay was conducted using the mixed positive standard and nucleic acids of ALV-E, ALV-K, MDV, FAdV-4, H6N6 AIV, E. coli, and S. pullorum, to confirm that amplification occurs exclusively in the presence of the target templates. Sensitivity was assessed using serial dilutions of the mixed positive standard ranging from 1 × 106 to 1 × 101 copies/μL to determine the lowest detectable template concentration. Reproducibility was evaluated at 1 × 107, 1 × 105, and 1 × 103 copies/μL using three intra- and inter-assay replicates. Intra-assay variation was evaluated within the same run, while inter-assay variation was assessed across independent runs using standards from different batches. The coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated as CV = (SD / x̄) × 100%, where SD is the standard deviation and x̄ is the mean Ct value.No template controls (NTC) were included in all experiments to monitor for potential contamination.

Coinfection simulation

To assess the suitability of the established M-qPCR assay for detecting co-infections, mixtures of ALV-J, REV, and CIAV positive standards at two concentration levels were prepared in various combinations. These templates were used to evaluate potential competitive inhibition and assay performance when large concentration differences existed among targets.

Clinical sample collection, processing and detection

Collection of samples

From May to August 2025, a total of 40 Puding high-legged chickens, 48 Xianglushan chickens, and 30 Qianshantun chickens—local breeds in Guizhou Province with unknown health status—were randomly sampled. For each bird, both serum and cloacal swab specimens were collected and transported to the laboratory under chilled conditions.a. Serum Samples

For serum collection, approximately 2 mL of blood was aseptically drawn from the wing vein into plain tubes, allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 min, and centrifuged at 3,000 r/min for 10 min; the supernatant was collected as serum (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China, 2016).b. Swabs Samples

Cloacal swabs were rotated three times inside the cloaca and gently touched with fecal material, then placed into sterile tubes containing 1.5 mL PBS supplemented with penicillin (100 IU/mL) and streptomycin (100 μg/mL). The suspensions were centrifuged at 10,000 r/min for 5 min at 4 ℃, and the supernatants were filtered through 0.22 μm disposable filters (Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China, 2016).

Following the above procedures, a total of 118 serum samples and 118 cloacal swab samples were collected from the three chicken breeds.

Nucleic acid extraction

Viral nucleic acids were extracted using the MiniBEST Viral RNA/DNA Extraction Kit Ver.5.0 (Takara) for subsequent sample detection.

Validation of the M-qPCR assay

To validate the reliability of the developed M-qPCR assay, all serum samples were also tested in parallel using three commercial TaqMan-based singleplex qPCR kits: ALV-J (Zhengzhou Zhongdao Biotechnology, China), REV (Wuhan Keqian Biology, China), and CIAV (Wuhan Keqian Biology, China). All assays were performed according to the manufacturers’ instructions, and the results were compared with those from the M-qPCR method to assess diagnostic concordance.

Statistical analysis

For experiments with a repetition in duplicate or more (n ≥ 2), results such as Ct value and plasmid concentration are expressed as arithmetic means. The standard curve equation, coefficient of determination (R2), and amplification efficiency (E), as well as SD for repeatability analysis, were calculated using Microsoft Excel 2021. Numerical data were rounded to one or two decimal places according to measurement precision.

Optimization and performance evaluation of the M-qPCR assay

Based on the optimized S-qPCR results, the M-qPCR assay was further refined using a matrix approach for reaction components and a temperature gradient for cycling conditions. Optimization was guided by the lowest average Ct values across all three fluorescence channels. The final 25 μL reaction system included 12.5 μL of 2× Premix Ex Taq (Probe qPCR), 0.5 μL of each mixed primer (10 μM), 1 μL of mixed probe (10 μM), 2 μL of DNA template, and 8.5 μL of sterilized ddH2O. The optimized cycling conditions were: 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 56.7 °C for 30 s.

Establishment of the standard curve for the M-qPCR assay

The standard curves for ALV-J, REV, and CIAV demonstrated good amplification performance (Fig. 3). All replicates showed standard deviations below 0.15, and no amplification was observed in the NTC. Discussion

Breeder-derived pathogens in chickens are characterized by vertical and horizontal transmission, often accompanied by immunosuppression, which facilitates their rapid spread through the pyramidal breeding structure of modern poultry production. This poses significant challenges for disease control and causes substantial economic losses (Li and Liu, 2014; Ma et al., 2024). Among the major BDPs, ALV-J, REV, and CIAV are of particular concern. Because coinfection among ALV-J, REV, and CIAV may lead to genetic recombination or mutation (Davidson and Silva, 2008), which can enable viral variants to escape detection, selecting conserved genomic regions as targets for primer and probe design is critical for the successful development of an M-qPCR assay. Previous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of using multiple sequence alignment to extract conserved sequences for diagnostic primer design (Chauhan et al., 2021; Ameen et al., 2022). For instance, Jeong et al. successfully identified conserved genomic regions of the rapidly evolving SARS-CoV-2 and detected nine RNA variants through RT-qPCR validation (Jeong et al., 2022). In this study, a total of 19 gp85 gene sequences of ALV-J, 9 gp90 gene sequences of REV, and 11 VP3 gene sequences of CIAV were retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database and used as targets for primer and TaqMan probe design to minimize the risk of variant escape and ensure reliable detection.

TaqMan probe-based qPCR supports multi-channel fluorescence output and enables the simultaneous detection of multiple targets in a single tube, making it an ideal choice for developing multiplex detection systems (Hawkins and Guest, 2017; Liu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). In this study, we developed an M-qPCR assay for the simultaneous detection of three major BDPs in chickens: ALV-J, REV, and CIAV. The assay demonstrated excellent linearity (R2 > 0.99) (Fig. 3), robust repeatability (intra-/inter-assay CV < 5%) (Table 3), and high specificity, with no cross-reactivity observed with common non-target pathogens such as ALV-E, MDV, and AIV (Fig. 4). The assay exhibited high sensitivity, with the lowest detectable template concentration determined to be 10 copies/μL (Fig. 5). Moreover, it reliably identified mixed infections even under unbalanced template concentrations (Fig. 6). The results showed strong agreement with those obtained from three commercial TaqMan-based S-qPCR kits (Table 6). Compared with various S-qPCR and multiplex M-qPCR assays developed for avian pathogen detection, the method developed in this study offers comparable or higher sensitivity while maintaining high efficiency and cost-effectiveness (Li et al., 2020; Kannaki et al., 2021; Dou et al., 2023). Overall, this M-qPCR assay is suitable for investigating the infection and co-infection status of these three viruses in clinical samples.

Nowadays, the eradication of BDPs in China mainly relies on pathogen detection to remove positive individuals and establish entirely negative breeder flocks (Tan et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024). Among these, ALV-J is commonly screened using ELISA, a method that is labor-intensive and time-consuming (Chen et al., 2024). To improve the efficiency of eradication, various alternative methods have been explored. For example, Qian et al. developed a peptide ELISA based on a novel B-cell epitope, which demonstrated higher sensitivity than commercial ELISA kits (Chen et al., 2024); Yu et al. developed a colloidal gold strip test targeting the p27 antigen, which showed high sensitivity and specificity and was highly consistent with ELISA results (Yu et al., 2019); Li et al. identified LMH cell lines, which better support ALV-J proliferation, thereby shortening the detection cycle (Li et al., 2018). However, these methods primarily focus on ALV-J itself, neglecting the synergistic interference from other viruses such as CIAV and REV (Li et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021). Previous studies have shown that CIAV and REV can significantly reduce ALV-J eradication efficiency by inducing immunosuppression (Cui et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024). Therefore, simultaneous detection of multiple pathogens is crucial during the eradication process. In this study, we developed a M-qPCR assay capable of simultaneously detecting ALV-J, CIAV, and REV, providing significant practical value and potential for widespread application.

Detection results often vary among different sample types collected from the same population (Wu et al., 2020). Therefore, proper selection of sample type is crucial to minimize the risk of false-negative results. In avian pathogen detection, serum and cloacal swabs are the most commonly used materials (Stokes et al., 2020; Fagrach et al., 2023), although semen, egg albumen, and oropharyngeal swabs have also been reported (Li et al., 2017; Killian, 2020). In this study, only serum and cloacal swabs were compared due to limited resources. Serum yielded lower Ct values and higher detection rates (Fig. 7A, 7B; Table 5), indicating it was more suitable for the developed assay. This result may be attributed to the higher viral load and lower interference typically found in serum samples.

ALV-J is highly pathogenic, rapidly spreading, and widely distributed, with reports from many countries and regions (Fandino et al., 2023). As one of the countries most severely affected by ALV-J, China’s poultry industry has suffered significant losses multiple times (Zhou et al., 2019). In this study, the serum positivity rate for ALV-J in three local chicken breeds from Guizhou was 33.9% (40/118), higher than that reported in large-scale farms in the same province (Liu et al., 2022). This rate was comparable to that of the Qiandongnan Xiaoxiang chicken (42.7%, 192/450)(Wang et al., 2023), suggesting that local breeds may have a higher prevalence of ALV-J than commercial poultry, consistent with previous studies (Chen et al., 2021). In addition to ALV-J, this study identified REV (9.3%, 11/118) and CIAV (7.6%, 9/118), providing updated epidemiological data on local chicken populations in Guizhou. Co-infection with multiple viruses is a common phenomenon in nature, and during co-infection, the biological characteristics such as viral replication, pathological changes, host range, and infection rate can all be altered (Mascia and Gallitelli, 2016). ALV-J, REV, and CIAV share highly similar epidemiological characteristics, and cases of coinfection with ALV-J have been reported (Cui et al., 2009; Xu et al., 2024). Here, mixed infections accounted for 20.4% of all positives, all associated with ALV-J, indicating that ALV-J not only plays a dominant role in single infections but also serves as the central pathogen in mixed infections. This suggests that, in the control and prevention of ALV-J, attention must also be given to the potential coinfection with viruses like CIAV to prevent more severe losses.

Notably, qPCR, with its high sensitivity, enables pooling multiple samples in a single reaction, which significantly reduces both detection costs and labor intensity (Mathevon et al., 2019).Previous studies have combined qPCR with sample pooling strategies to establish a blood nucleic acid screening method for ALV-J, which offers a short detection cycle, low cost, and high sensitivity, making it particularly suitable for rapid screening of large chicken populations in low-prevalence settings (Dong et al., 2024). Therefore, building upon the detection methods established in this study, further development of multiplex pooling assays for pathogen eradication will be a key direction for future research.
