Paper: PMC5359238.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- No specific optimal, range, or survival temperatures were provided.
- No mention of thermophilic, psychrophilic, mesophilic, heat tolerance, cold tolerance, or temperature-adapted enzymes/membranes was found.

Environmental Context:
- The isolates were recovered from cattle herds (ruminants) and were associated with enteric infections in cattle.

Concise Summary:
The study focuses on the genomic plasticity and horizontal gene transfer (HGT) of O-antigen gene clusters (O-AGC) in Shiga toxin-producing *Escherichia coli* (STEC) strains isolated from cattle. The analysis compares genetic differences between serotypes O156:H25, O182:H25, and O156:H8, demonstrating that while core virulence and flagellar genes are highly conserved, the O-AGC regions show significant variation, suggesting recent serotype switching. The research integrates molecular data (whole-genome sequencing, MLST) with epidemiological context from longitudinal sampling in cattle herds. The findings emphasize the importance of O-antigen switching for host colonization and immune evasion, suggesting that such genomic plasticity can be used for improved risk assessment of these zoonotic pathogens. No data regarding the thermal physiology or adaptation of the isolates were presented.
