Concise Summary:
This study focuses on developing and validating a deep learning model (CNN4Essential) for predicting bacterial essential genes by fusing multi-source genomic data (sequence, PPI, GO annotation, subcellular localization). The model's performance was evaluated using intra-species (INSP) and leave-a-species-out (LASP) cross-validation across 22 prokaryotic species. The methodology involves feature engineering, weighted fusion using Random Forest importance, and dimensionality reduction via Truncated SVD, followed by CNN classification. The model demonstrated superior predictive accuracy compared to existing methods, particularly in INSP (average AUC of 0.884). A case study on one species demonstrated the model's utility in prioritizing high-confidence, druggable antimicrobial targets. The analysis is purely computational and does not report any physiological data related to temperature, thermal adaptation, or growth conditions.
