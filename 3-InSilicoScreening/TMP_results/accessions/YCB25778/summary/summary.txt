Concise Summary:
Antarctic microorganisms were isolated from extreme environments including soil, moss, permafrost, and marine sediment. These isolates exhibit strong adaptations to cold and freezing conditions, demonstrating survival at -80 °C and functional cryoprotective activity at -18 °C. Optimal growth conditions utilized in the study ranged from 4 °C to 25 °C, with specific protein extraction protocols optimized for 15 °C. The organisms are characterized as psychrophilic/cold-adapted. Furthermore, the extracted extracellular proteins demonstrated notable thermal stability, resisting denaturation at 100 °C. The study confirms the relevance of these psychrophilic microbes as sources of biomacromolecules for cryopreservation applications across prokaryotic, eukaryotic, and viral systems.
