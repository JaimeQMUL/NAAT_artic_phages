Paper: PMC12533011.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal activity observed at pH 7.0 after one hour of incubation.
- Stable across a wide pH range (4 to 11) in both acidic and alkaline environments.
- Maintained high infectivity at −20, 4, 25, and 37 °C with no significant reduction in titer after 1 h of incubation.
- Infectivity remained high at mildly acidic (pH 5.0) and mildly alkaline (pH 9.0–10.0) conditions.
- Minimal reduction in titer observed at pH 6.0 and 8.0 (near-neutral range).
- Completely inactivated in extreme acidic (pH ≤3.0) or alkaline (pH ≥12.0) conditions.
- Maintained high infectivity at 37 °C (physiological temperature).
- Relatively stable at 40 °C, with a moderate decline observed at 50 °C.
- Marked loss of infectivity observed at 60 °C and above.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from a water sample (River Ganges).

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibits robust stability across a broad pH range (4–11), with optimal activity noted at pH 7.0. Thermostability is demonstrated across a wide range, maintaining high infectivity at −20 °C, 4 °C, 25 °C, and 37 °C. It shows moderate thermal tolerance up to 40 °C, but is inactivated at 60 °C and above. The phage is classified as stable under physiological conditions, suggesting mesophilic characteristics, and was isolated from a natural water source.
