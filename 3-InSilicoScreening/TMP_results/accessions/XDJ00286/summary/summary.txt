Paper: PMC13164475.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Biofilm formation was tested across a range of temperatures: 4 °C, 14 °C, 23 °C, and 37 °C.
- Biofilm formation was found to be significantly enhanced at 4 °C, particularly in the presence of a specific medium.
- Biofilm formation was generally observed to be more pronounced at higher temperatures (23 °C and 37 °C) compared to 14 °C.
- A significant difference in biofilm formation was noted between 23 °C and 37 °C, with 37 °C showing the highest overall biofilm formation potential in one set of assays.
- Biofilm degradation studies indicated that essential oils were most effective at 14 °C and 37 °C, and least effective at 23 °C.
- The study compared biofilm formation across different temperatures, suggesting temperature is a significant influencing factor.

Environmental Context:
- The isolates were primarily obtained from chicken feces and chicken appendix, indicating a food/animal-associated source.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated biofilm formation and essential oil efficacy against the pathogen across a temperature gradient (4 °C to 37 °C). Biofilm formation was found to be significantly influenced by temperature, with enhanced formation observed at 4 °C, 23 °C, and 37 °C, and the highest overall potential noted at 37 °C. The pathogen was isolated from poultry sources. The research utilized these temperature variations to test the antibacterial and biofilm-modulating effects of essential oils, noting that biofilm degradation was most effective at 14 °C and 37 °C, suggesting temperature-dependent physiological responses related to biofilm matrix stability and antimicrobial action.
