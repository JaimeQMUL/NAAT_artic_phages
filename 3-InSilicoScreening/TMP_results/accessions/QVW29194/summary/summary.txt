- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from sewage of a commercial pig farm.

- Enzymes adapted to temperature: Not specified.
- Membrane adaptations: Not specified.
- Thermal stress responses: High stability was noted at elevated temperatures up to 60°C.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage from a commercial pig farm.

Concise Summary:
The phage was isolated from sewage and demonstrated robust stability characteristics relevant for food applications. Optimal bacterial growth was observed at $37^{\circ}\text{C}$. The phage itself exhibited high thermal stability, retaining infectivity up to $60^{\circ}\text{C}$ for 60 minutes, while losing viability at $\ge 70^{\circ}\text{C}$. The phage demonstrated significant activity and stability in cold environments, effectively reducing bacterial counts in milk at $4^{\circ}\text{C}$ over extended periods (up to 7 days), suggesting good cold tolerance. Furthermore, the phage maintained strong bactericidal activity in solid food matrices (raw beef and fresh lettuce) at both $4^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $25^{\circ}\text{C}$, with the most pronounced and sustained effect observed at refrigeration temperatures. The overall profile suggests suitability for food preservation processes involving refrigeration or ambient storage conditions.
