Paper: PMC13164428.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- **Staphylococci:** Growth is generally observed between 7 °C and 48 °C. The decline in counts during ripening suggests a sensitivity to temperature changes, while the process of cooking (reaching temperatures above 80 °C) was noted to inactivate these bacteria.
- **Listeria:** Can replicate under refrigeration and survive in high salt concentrations and low water activity. The ability to form biofilms allows persistence in the processing environment, suggesting tolerance to sanitation procedures and desiccation.
- **E. coli:** The study notes that the cooking process (reaching temperatures above 80 °C) eliminates remaining strains. The general discussion implies growth across typical food handling temperatures.
- **General:** The text mentions that the general growth range for *S. aureus* is between 7 °C and 48 °C.

Environmental Context:
- **Sources:** The organisms are associated with the environment, originating from the gut flora of humans and animals, and contamination can occur from the udder skin, milking equipment, or operators’ hands. The study context is raw milk dairy products from alpine dairies, which are remote, high-altitude settings.

Concise Summary:
Microbiological analysis of raw milk dairy products revealed pathogen dynamics influenced by processing and storage temperatures. Coagulase-positive Staphylococci exhibit growth potential between 7 °C and 48 °C, with counts declining significantly during cheese ripening, suggesting temperature-dependent reduction. The inactivation of these bacteria was confirmed by exposure to temperatures exceeding 80 °C (e.g., in ricotta production). *Listeria* species demonstrate notable survival capabilities under refrigeration, high salt, and low water activity, facilitated by biofilm formation in processing environments. *E. coli* strains are susceptible to thermal inactivation above 80 °C. The overall context involves contamination risks originating from the environment and farm/processing stages, with the high-altitude, remote nature of the production sites being a key environmental factor. No explicit classification (e.g., psychrophilic, mesophilic) was provided for the pathogens, but the observed survival and growth patterns are consistent with mesophilic organisms adapted to refrigerated food matrices.
