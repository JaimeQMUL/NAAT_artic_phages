Paper: PMC13064462.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but assays were performed at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Phages retained titers after incubation at 4 °C, 37 °C, 50 °C, 60 °C, and 70 °C; PJNB030 and PJNB032 retained titers after 80 °C for 60 min.
- Incubation conditions: Assays were performed at 37 °C for various durations (e.g., 1 h for pH stability, 12 h for plaque assay, 72 h for biofilm assay).
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from a contaminated fermentation broth.
- Thermophilic adaptation: No explicit mention.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: No explicit mention.
- Mesophilic growth: Implied by optimal growth at 37 °C.
- Heat tolerance: Demonstrated by the retention of phage titers after exposure to 80 °C for 60 min for two of the three phages.
- Cold tolerance: Assays were performed at 4 °C.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: Not discussed.
- Membrane adaptations: Not discussed.
- Thermal stress responses: Demonstrated by phage survival at elevated temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/source: Contaminated fermentation broth.

Concise Summary:
Three phages were isolated from a contaminated fermentation broth. Phenotypic characterization revealed that the phages maintained stability across a broad pH range (pH 4–10). Thermal stability assays showed that while one phage was inactivated at 80 °C for 40 min, the other two phages retained measurable titers even after prolonged exposure to 80 °C (up to 60 min). The phages were tested for stability at 4 °C, 37 °C, 50 °C, 60 °C, and 70 °C. Optimal growth and lytic activity were observed at 37 °C. The phages exhibit significant heat tolerance, suggesting resilience beyond typical mesophilic conditions. The study utilized these thermal characteristics to highlight the limitations of conventional physical decontamination methods.
