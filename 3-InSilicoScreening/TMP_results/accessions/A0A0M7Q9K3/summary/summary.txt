Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for initial bacterial growth and phage incubation).
- Growth temperature range: Stable at 4–50 °C (for 1 hour incubation).
- Survival temperature range: Stable at 4–50 °C; titer decreased by half at 70 °C; essentially inactivated at 80 °C.
- Incubation conditions: Optimal MOI determination and initial bacterial growth were performed at 37 °C with shaking at 180 rpm.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from farm sewage.
- Mentioned adaptations: Good temperature tolerance and acid–base resistance were noted. The phage was found to be stable at 4–50 °C, suggesting good cold tolerance relative to higher temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage from underground wells around Urumqi city.

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophage was isolated from farm sewage. Its optimal growth and initial bacterial culture conditions were established at 37 °C. Regarding thermal stability, the phage demonstrated good tolerance, remaining stable at 4–50 °C for one hour. Titer reduction was observed at 70 °C (50% decrease) and significant inactivation at 80 °C. The authors explicitly noted its good temperature tolerance, which is beneficial for cold chain transportation and storage. The phage was not classified as thermophilic or psychrophilic, but its stability profile suggests robust performance within the mesophilic range relevant to the gastrointestinal tract (pH 4–12 stability was also noted, suggesting GI tolerance).
