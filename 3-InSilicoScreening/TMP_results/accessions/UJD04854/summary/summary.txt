Concise Summary:
The study investigated the transfer of antibiotic resistance genes in *E. coli* strains isolated from raw milk and dairy farm environments under heavy metal stress. The research focused on the molecular mechanisms of co-resistance, specifically demonstrating that low-to-moderate $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ concentrations (1-10 $\mu\text{g/L}$) significantly enhanced intrageneric conjugative transfer frequency, suggesting that oxidative stress induction drives horizontal gene transfer. Mechanistically, $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ treatment was shown to increase cell aggregation, decrease negative surface charge, and improve membrane permeability, modulating stress pathways like metal efflux and the SOS response. No data regarding the optimal, growth, or survival temperatures, or any specific thermal adaptations (e.g., mesophilic, thermophilic) of the isolated strains were presented.
