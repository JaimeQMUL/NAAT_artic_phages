Paper: PMC13209632.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for initial bacterial growth and phage incubation).
- Growth temperature range: Stable at 4–50 °C (for 1 hour incubation).
- Survival temperature range: Stable at 4–50 °C; titer decreased by half at 70 °C; essentially inactivated at 80 °C.
- Incubation conditions: Incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 180 rpm for various assays.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from farm sewage.
- Mentioned adaptations: Good temperature tolerance and acid–base resistance.
- Explicit classification: None provided.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Farm sewage.

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophage was isolated from farm sewage. Its optimal growth and stability were observed at 37 °C, remaining stable over a range of 4–50 °C for one hour. The phage demonstrated good temperature tolerance, showing a significant titer decrease at 70 °C and near-inactivation at 80 °C. The phage was also found to be stable across a wide pH range (pH 4–12). These thermal characteristics suggest suitability for storage and survival in various environmental conditions, including the gastrointestinal tract, which is beneficial for its potential use in treating diarrhea.
