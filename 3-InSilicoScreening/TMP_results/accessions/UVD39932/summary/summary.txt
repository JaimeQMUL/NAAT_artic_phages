Concise Summary:
The research details a novel class of phage-derived effectors associated with contractile injection systems (CISs) found widely in Actinobacteria. These effectors, related to phage tape measure proteins, are packaged within a nanoparticle structure and translocated to lipid membranes. The function of these effectors is to modify the physiological properties of the host bacterium, enhancing its adaptation to microbial competition. The study highlights the structural and functional diversity of these effectors, demonstrating that they can reprogram phenotypic responsiveness to neighboring bacteria. The findings establish phage elements as reconfigurable platforms for bacterial adaptation to environmental conditions, suggesting a role in community ecology, but provide no explicit data regarding the thermal niche or temperature adaptations of the involved organisms.

Thermal Evidence:

- Thermoresistance of submerged spores was noted, and this thermoresistance was compared to aerial spores, but no quantitative temperature data for survival was given.

Environmental Context:
- No environmental source or habitat was mentioned.

Concise Summary:
Sporulation of several species of *Streptomyces* was observed in submerged liquid cultures following nutritional downshift. Sporulation was specifically triggered by phosphate nutritional downshift, induced by the addition of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$. The viability and thermoresistance of the resulting submerged spores were confirmed to be comparable to aerial spores. The formation of spore-shaped bodies under $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and proline supplementation resulted in a thermoresistance profile distinct from aerial spores. No explicit temperature parameters (optimal, range, or survival) were provided for thermal classification.
