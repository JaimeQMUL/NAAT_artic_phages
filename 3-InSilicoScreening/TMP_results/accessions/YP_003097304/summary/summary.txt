Paper: PMC12838246.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Temperature abuse was simulated at 25°C for 24 hours, mimicking post-harvest handling under warm ambient conditions.
- The study noted that *Vibrio* species can increase up to 100-fold within 10 hours when stored at ambient temperatures.
- Increased levels of *Vibrionaceae* were observed as the temperature increased during commercial transport and storage.
- The analysis of *Vibrio* levels showed that the stable conditions in bottom cages may support populations that remain metabolically active, potentially leading to higher counts in enrichment methods.
- Post-harvest temperature abuse provided favorable conditions for *Vibrio* proliferation directly within oyster tissues.

Environmental Context:
- The organisms were studied in the context of oyster aquaculture systems (bottom and floating cages) in coastal bay waters, with sampling occurring from June to November 2019.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated microbial dynamics in oysters across different aquaculture practices (bottom vs. floating cages) and post-harvest conditions. Thermal stress was simulated by exposing oysters to 25°C for 24 hours, which was noted to favor *Vibrio* proliferation. General observations indicated that *Vibrio* levels increase significantly with rising ambient temperatures during storage. The stable environment of bottom cages was associated with metabolically active populations, while post-harvest temperature abuse was shown to favor *Vibrio* proliferation within the oyster tissues. The research utilized these temperature variables to assess the impact of handling and environmental conditions on the overall microbial community structure and pathogen load.
