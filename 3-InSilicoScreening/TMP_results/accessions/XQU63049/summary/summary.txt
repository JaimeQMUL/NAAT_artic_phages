Paper: PMC13120357.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature for the host bacterium was 28 °C.
- The phage remained active across a temperature range of 20 °C to 50 °C.
- The phage was completely inactivated at temperatures above 60 °C.
- Thermal stability testing was conducted at 4 °C, 28 °C, 40 °C, 50 °C, 60 °C, and 70 °C for 1 h.
- The phage showed stability at 20–50 °C, suggesting compatibility with moderate soil temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- The phage was isolated from wastewater and soil samples.
- The study notes that the phage's stability characteristics (20–50 °C) suggest practical compatibility with field conditions in subtropical regions with moderate soil temperatures.

Concise Summary:
Characterization of the bacteriophage revealed that the optimal growth temperature for its bacterial host was 28 °C. The phage itself demonstrated activity across a temperature range of 20 °C to 50 °C, with complete inactivation observed above 60 °C. Thermal stability testing was performed across 4 °C to 70 °C. The phage was isolated from environmental sources, specifically wastewater and soil. The observed stability profile suggests mesophilic characteristics, indicating potential compatibility with moderate field temperatures, although formulation strategies may be needed to enhance environmental persistence.

Paper: PMC10537231.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 28°C
- Growth temperature range: 28°C (implied by optimal growth)
- Survival temperature range: 30°C to 50°C (no negative effect after 1h incubation); viability maintained at 55°C (85% survival) and 60°C (40% survival); completely lost at 65°C and above.
- Incubation conditions: Various temperatures tested (30°C to 75°C) for 1 hour.
- Environmental source/habitat: Sewage samples.
- Adaptation notes: No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was given. The phage demonstrated moderate heat tolerance, with viability significantly decreasing above 50°C.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage samples.

Concise Summary:
The phage was isolated from sewage samples. Optimal growth and activity were observed at 28°C. Thermal stability testing indicated that the phage maintained infectivity after incubation at temperatures ranging from 30°C to 50°C for one hour. While viability was significantly reduced at 55°C (85% survival) and 60°C (40% survival), infectivity was completely lost at 65°C and higher. The phage exhibits moderate heat tolerance, suggesting it is adapted to mesophilic conditions, as no extreme temperature adaptations were noted.
