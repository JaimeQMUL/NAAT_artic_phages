Paper: 28395824.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Heat shock conditions tested: 68°C for 20 minutes (optimal recovery observed).
- Heat shock conditions tested: 15.61 and 15.24 logCFU/mL recovery observed under these conditions.
- Significant count reduction observed after heat shock at 80°C for 10 minutes (P≤0.05).
- Survival during cooking process: 3-4 log reduction observed in raw batter.
- Survival during cold storage: Counts remained >10⁶ CFU/g.

Environmental Context:
- No explicit environmental source/habitat mentioned.
- No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) given by the authors.

Concise Summary:
Spore-forming bacteria demonstrated significant survival capabilities during food processing and storage. Optimal spore recovery was noted following a heat shock of 68°C for 20 minutes. However, a significant reduction in spore counts was observed after exposure to 80°C for 10 minutes. The bacteria maintained viable counts (>10⁶ CFU/g) throughout the entire cold storage period, and their survival through the cooking process, despite a 3-4 log reduction, highlights their robustness in functional cooked food products. The data strongly support their capacity for heat tolerance and cold tolerance within the context of food preservation.
