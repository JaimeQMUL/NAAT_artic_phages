Paper: PMC10194148.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal storage temperature identified as 4 °C for phage products.
- Storage stability was tested across multiple temperatures: 4 °C, 28 °C, -20 °C, and -80 °C.
- At 28 °C, phage titers dropped rapidly in standard buffer but were significantly stabilized by the addition of hydroxyapatite (HAP).
- For cryopreservation, 10% DMSO was found to be highly effective at protecting phage titers at both -20 °C and -80 °C, showing superior protection compared to glycerol or standard buffer.
- Lyophilized phages showed excellent long-term storage stability at 4 °C, with no significant titer drop observed over 12 months.
- At 28 °C, lyophilized phages with excipients (TSB, tryptone/SMP + trehalose) maintained stability significantly better than initial phages, though titers still declined over 12 months.
- Accelerated stability testing demonstrated phage inactivation kinetics across a range of elevated temperatures (50–70 °C), confirming temperature-dependent inactivation.
- The analysis of activation energy ($E_a$) suggested differences in thermal sensitivity between phage types (e.g., Myoviridae vs. Podoviridae).

Environmental Context:
- The phages were isolated from environmental sources, specifically associated with marine/aquaculture environments (implied by the source of the bacteria).

Concise Summary:
The study extensively investigated the thermal stability and shelf-life prediction for bacteriophages. Optimal storage conditions were determined to be 4 °C for ambient storage, while cryopreservation at -20 °C and -80 °C was best supported by 10% DMSO. At room temperature (28 °C), stability was significantly enhanced by incorporating HAP or excipient combinations (protein/saccharide) during lyophilization. The phages exhibited temperature-dependent inactivation kinetics, which were successfully modeled using the Accelerated Stability Assessment Program (ASAP) and the Arrhenius equation, providing a reliable method for predicting shelf-life across various temperatures. The findings highlight that formulation strategies (HAP, DMSO, excipients) are critical for maintaining phage viability across different thermal regimes, suggesting robust preservation potential for biopharmaceutical development.
