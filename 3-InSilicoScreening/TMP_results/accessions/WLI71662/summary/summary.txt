Paper: PMC13147348.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37°C
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but optimal growth was at 37°C.
- Survival temperature range: Tested survival at 4°C, 25°C, 37°C, and 45°C (storage); Heat resistance tested at 70°C, 80°C, and 95°C (feed processing).
- Incubation conditions: 37°C for 24h (initial culture); 37°C for 48h (sporulation).
- Thermophilic adaptation: One strain originated from a thermophilic hot spring habitat.
- Heat tolerance: Spores maintained high survival rates at 80°C and 95°C when coencapsulated with the polysaccharide matrix, demonstrating suitability for feed processing temperatures (70°C–95°C).
- Cold tolerance: Viability was tested at 4°C, showing significant decline over 60 days, but survival was maintained at 4°C compared to other storage conditions.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: Not specified.
- Membrane adaptations: Not specified.
- Thermal stress responses: The coencapsulation matrix significantly enhanced survival rates under high heat stress compared to alginate-only encapsulation.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolates were obtained from diverse sources including shrimp pond soil, hot spring soil, chicken manure, and rubber latex serum. One strain was explicitly noted as originating from a thermophilic hot spring habitat.

Concise Summary:
The study utilized a multistrain consortium of spore-forming bacteria, including strains isolated from diverse environments such as hot springs, soil, and manure. The optimal growth temperature for culturing and sporulation was consistently maintained at 37°C. The bacteria exhibit inherent resilience, with one strain noted to originate from a thermophilic hot spring habitat. The primary focus regarding thermal stress was the assessment of spore survival during simulated feed processing, where the coencapsulation matrix (alginate + polysaccharide) significantly enhanced thermal tolerance, maintaining high viability at elevated temperatures (e.g., 80°C and 95°C) compared to alginate-only encapsulation. Furthermore, the encapsulated spores demonstrated good shelf stability across a range of storage temperatures, including 4°C, 25°C, 37°C, and 45°C, with the composite matrix providing superior protection over 60 days compared to alginate-only beads. This robust thermal profile confirms the suitability of the formulation for incorporation into commercial feed production processes.
