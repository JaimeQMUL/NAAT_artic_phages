Paper: PMC9319786.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for bacterial growth and phage activity).
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but optimal activity observed at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Stable at 4 °C and -70 °C (with or without glycerol) over six months. Activity was completely lost at 37 °C after 6 months of storage, though initial activity was maintained at 37 °C for short-term assays.
- Incubation conditions: Assays were performed at 37 °C for lytic activity and adsorption studies. Long-term stability studies were conducted at 37 °C, 22–24 °C (room temperature), 4 °C, and -70 °C.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from environmental samples (wastewater, rivers) and clinical samples (urine, wounds, etc.).
- Adaptation/Tolerance: Demonstrated stability at 4 °C and -70 °C. Activity loss was noted at 37 °C over extended periods (6 months).

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater, rivers, and clinical sites (urine, wounds).

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophages were characterized using optimal growth conditions at 37 °C for both bacterial growth and lytic assays. Stability studies revealed that the phages retained activity over six months when stored at 4 °C or frozen at -70 °C (with or without glycerol). Conversely, storage at 37 °C resulted in a complete loss of activity over the same period. The phages were isolated from diverse sources, including environmental samples (wastewater, rivers) and clinical specimens (urine, wounds). No explicit classification regarding thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic adaptation was provided; however, the optimal activity was consistently observed at 37 °C, while long-term storage stability was best maintained at refrigeration or frozen temperatures.
