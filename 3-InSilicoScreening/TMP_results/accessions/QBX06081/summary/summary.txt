Concise Summary:
The study describes a marine bacterium exhibiting potent, selective algicidal activity against a toxic dinoflagellate. The physiological mechanism involves a dose-dependent dual-mode strategy: indirect, secretion-mediated inhibition at low bacterial densities, transitioning to rapid, contact-dependent lysis at high densities. Genomic analysis revealed that host contact triggers the immediate upregulation of the Type VI Secretion System (VgrG), followed by Quorum Sensing activation and massive induction of secretory machinery. Host-specific adhesion is linked to a rhamnosyl polysaccharide biosynthesis gene cluster. Biogeographic analysis confirms the strain's ecological adaptation to eutrophic conditions.
