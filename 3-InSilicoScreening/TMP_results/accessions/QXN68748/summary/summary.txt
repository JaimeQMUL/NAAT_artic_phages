Paper: PMC10671443.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 28 °C (for plaque formation and one-step growth).
- Growth temperature range: Stable at 4–55 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Robust within 4–45 °C; rapidly decreases to zero at temperatures >65 °C.
- Incubation conditions: Tested at 4, 25, 35, 45, 55, 65, and 75 °C.
- Heat tolerance: Demonstrated stability up to 55 °C, showing better thermal tolerance than several previously characterized phages (e.g., vB_VhaM_pir03, vB_VhaS_MAG7, vB-Vhas-R18L).
- Cold tolerance: Stable at 4 °C.
- Thermophilic/Psychrophilic adaptation: No explicit classification given, but stability across a wide range suggests adaptability.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Seawater from a scallop-farming area in China.
- Extreme Environment Isolation: Not explicitly stated as extreme, but isolated from a marine/aquaculture environment.

Concise Summary:
This lytic phage was characterized in a marine/aquaculture setting. Its optimal growth and plaque formation occurred at 28 °C. The phage exhibits a broad stability profile, remaining robust across a temperature range of 4–45 °C, and maintaining activity up to 55 °C. It showed rapid inactivation above 65 °C. The phage's stability profile suggests good thermal resilience for practical applications, outperforming several previously reported phages in terms of thermal tolerance. The phage was found to be stable across a pH range of 4–10.

Paper: PMC13073097.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Growth was tested across a range of temperatures: 4 °C, room temperature (RT $\approx$ 20 °C), 30 °C, 37 °C, and 42 °C.
- The organism demonstrated capability to grow across the evaluated temperature range (4–42 °C).
- Higher optical densities were achieved in a specific rich medium compared to another rich medium, suggesting optimal growth conditions are medium-dependent.
- The organism was observed to tolerate salt concentrations ranging from 3% to 5% NaCl.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from raw milk cheeses (Brie, Camembert, Reblochon) and found to be absent in pasteurized milk-derived cheeses.

Concise Summary:
The organism exhibits a broad growth temperature range, successfully growing between 4 °C and 42 °C. Growth rates and optimal conditions are influenced by the culture medium, with higher biomass observed in one specific rich medium compared to another. The organism demonstrated tolerance to moderate salt concentrations (3–5% NaCl). It was consistently isolated from raw milk cheeses, suggesting an adaptation to the complex, variable thermal and chemical conditions of artisanal dairy fermentation and ripening processes. The authors did not explicitly classify the organism as thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic, but the tested range encompasses mesophilic conditions (30–37 °C) and extends into psychrotolerant/psychrotrophic ranges (4 °C) and slightly higher temperatures (42 °C). The ability to grow across this wide range suggests significant thermal adaptability within the cheese matrix.
