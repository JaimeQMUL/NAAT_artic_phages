Concise Summary:
The study investigated the induction of a viable-but-non-culturable (VBNC) state in an adherent-invasive *E. coli* strain when exposed to cupric gluconate. The induction of this VBNC state was explicitly noted to be temperature-dependent. While the paper details metabolic shifts (repression of energy metabolism, enhanced outer-membrane maintenance, Fe-S cluster repair) and susceptibility changes (tolerance to ciprofloxacin, susceptibility to ampicillin/tetracycline), it does not provide quantitative data regarding optimal growth temperature, specific growth temperature ranges, or defined survival temperature ranges. The authors did not classify the organism based on thermal adaptation (e.g., mesophile, thermophile). The primary focus remains on metal redox imbalance and oxidative stress mechanisms driving the VBNC program, with temperature acting as a modulating factor rather than a defining characteristic of the organism's thermal niche.
