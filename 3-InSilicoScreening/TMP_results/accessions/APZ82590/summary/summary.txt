Paper: PMC5490822.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal preservation temperatures were noted at 4 °C and 21 °C for LB-medium or TMK-buffer.
- Cryopreservation at -80 °C strongly reduced viability.
- Viability testing was conducted at 21 °C, 4 °C, and -80 °C.
- The highest viability was observed in TMK buffer, with stabilization at approximately 80% for the first virus at 4 °C and 45% at 21 °C, and for the second virus at 30% at 4 °C.
- Storage in LB medium at 21 °C stabilized at about 20% for the first virus and 30% for the second virus after 9 weeks.
- Storage in TN buffer resulted in significant instability, with up to 100% loss of potential after 3 weeks, except for the first virus at 21 °C.
- The general trend suggests better stability at lower, non-freezing temperatures (4 °C to 21 °C) compared to cryopreservation.

Environmental Context:
- The viruses were isolated from raw sewage.

Concise Summary:
The viruses were isolated from raw sewage. Physiological stability testing revealed that the viruses are most stable when stored in TMK buffer at 4 °C or 21 °C, with the first virus showing the highest viability (80% at 4 °C). Storage in LB medium at 21 °C maintained viability (20% for the first virus, 30% for the second virus) over 9 weeks. Conversely, storage in TN buffer led to rapid inactivation regardless of temperature. Cryopreservation at -80 °C was ineffective for long-term preservation. The data indicate a preference for moderate, non-freezing temperatures for optimal short-to-medium term viability, suggesting mesophilic adaptation rather than extreme thermophilic or psychrophilic specialization.
