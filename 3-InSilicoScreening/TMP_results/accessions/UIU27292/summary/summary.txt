Paper: PMC6723556.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (for biofilm formation).
- Growth temperature range: 22 °C and 37 °C (used for biofilm formation assays).
- Survival temperature range: Not explicitly stated.
- Incubation conditions: Aerobic incubation at 37 °C for initial isolation; Biofilm assays conducted at 22 °C or 37 °C.
- Environmental source/habitat: Cattle faecal samples.
- Mention of adaptations: The study notes that biofilm formation was more evident at 48 h compared to 24 h and 72 h at 22 °C and 37 °C, and that biofilm formation was strongest at 22 °C and 37 °C. The *rpoS* gene, a stress response sigma factor, was noted to be a strong predictor of biofilm-forming ability at both 22 °C and 37 °C, and that *rpoS* is a thermoregulator expressed more at 23 °C than at 37 °C, impacting biofilm-forming genes.
- Explicit classification: No explicit classification (e.g., mesophile) was given by the authors, but the growth conditions suggest mesophilic adaptation.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Cattle faecal samples from the North-West Province of South Africa.

Concise Summary:
The organism was isolated from cattle faecal samples. Growth and biofilm formation assays were conducted at 22 °C and 37 °C, with 37 °C being the optimal temperature for biofilm formation. The study highlighted the role of the *rpoS* gene, a stress response sigma factor, which is noted to be a thermoregulator expressed more at 23 °C than at 37 °C, suggesting adaptation to mesophilic conditions. Biofilm formation was observed to be strongest at both 22 °C and 37 °C, with biofilm formation being more evident at 48 h compared to 24 h or 72 h at either temperature.
