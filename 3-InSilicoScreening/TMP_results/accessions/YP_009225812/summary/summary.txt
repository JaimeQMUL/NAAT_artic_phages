Concise Summary:
The microbial community analyzed originates from circumneutral terrestrial hot springs in Mexico, exhibiting characteristics of a moderately thermophilic environment with sampled temperatures around 45–50 °C. The dominant bacterial populations are identified as sulfur-oxidizing bacteria belonging to genera such as *Rhodococcus*, *Acinetobacter*, *Thermomonas*, and *Bacillus*. The community structure is influenced by the physicochemical properties of the water, including high levels of carbonates and calcium. The organisms demonstrate adaptations to extreme conditions, evidenced by the presence of mechanisms to counteract oxidative stress and DNA damage. Functionally, the community is highly active in the sulfur cycle (sulfur oxidation/reduction) and carbon fixation, with the Calvin–Benson cycle identified as a primary metabolic pathway. The viral community is dominated by bacteriophages infecting prokaryotes, with the *Herelleviridae* family being notably abundant in this specific habitat. Overall, the findings confirm that the microbial structure is strongly dictated by the unique chemical composition and moderate thermal regime of the hot springs.

Paper: PMC13107908.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature is generally cited as 28°C.
- Tested temperature range for metabolic study was 24°C to 44°C.
- Enhanced metabolic activity (glucose consumption, metabolite production) was observed at elevated temperatures (e.g., 40°C for d-ribulose, 34°C for shinorine), even when cell viability was significantly reduced.
- The organism showed metabolic acceleration at elevated temperatures, independent of cell growth.
- The study suggests that optimal metabolite production temperatures can differ significantly from the optimal growth temperature.
- The organism exhibited changes in fatty acid composition (increased proportion of branched-chain fatty acids) when cultured at 40°C compared to 28°C, suggesting membrane adaptation to higher temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- The organism is a Gram-positive soil bacterium.
- No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was provided by the authors; the findings focus on metabolic responses across a range including elevated temperatures.

Concise Summary:
The organism, a Gram-positive soil bacterium, was studied across a temperature range of 24°C to 44°C, with an optimal growth temperature noted at 28°C. A key finding is that elevated temperatures (e.g., 40°C) significantly accelerate primary metabolism (glucose consumption) and the production of valuable metabolites (d-ribulose, shinorine), even when cell viability is severely compromised. This demonstrates that metabolic output can be decoupled from cell growth rates. The organism exhibits metabolic plasticity, with optimal production temperatures varying for different metabolites (e.g., 40°C for d-ribulose vs. 34°C for shinorine). Furthermore, temperature shifts induced measurable changes in cell membrane fatty acid composition, specifically an increase in branched-chain fatty acids at higher temperatures, suggesting a thermal adaptation mechanism influencing transport efficiency. The overall evidence points to the potential for temperature as a powerful, non-growth-related modulator of metabolic flux for bioproduction.
