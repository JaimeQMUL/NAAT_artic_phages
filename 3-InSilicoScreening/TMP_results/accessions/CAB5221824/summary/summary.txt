Concise Summary:
This study utilized sedaDNA from lake and marine sediments spanning the late Pleistocene and Holocene to reconstruct ancient viral communities. While no specific optimal growth temperatures were detailed, the analysis is framed by major climatic shifts, including glacial maxima and deglaciation. The data reveal temporal patterns suggesting that certain viral taxa exhibit higher relative abundances during colder glacial periods compared to warmer interglacials. Furthermore, the persistence of viral particles was noted to be enhanced under conditions of lower temperatures or reduced UV exposure, such as those found beneath frozen lake surfaces. The study also observed that the abundance of certain host lineages was associated with cold-adapted strains from polar regions. These findings indicate that ancient viral communities tracked significant environmental temperature fluctuations, suggesting that temperature gradients were key drivers shaping viral assemblages over millennial timescales.
