Paper: PMC12403702.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal culture temperature: 37°C.
- Growth temperature range: Optimal stability observed at 20–50°C.
- Survival temperature range: Good stability observed at 4°C, 21°C, 37°C, and 50°C. Significant titer decrease observed when temperature exceeded 50°C, with near complete inactivation at 70°C.
- Incubation conditions: Optimal plaque formation time was 6 hours, and the optimal culture temperature was 37°C.
- Thermal stress responses: The phage demonstrated good stability within the 4°C to 50°C range.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolated from the gut of *Periplaneta americana* L. (an insect).
- Classification: No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was given by the authors, but the optimal growth conditions suggest mesophilic adaptation, particularly around 37°C.

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophage was characterized with an optimal culture temperature of 37°C, and plaque formation was optimized at this temperature over 6 hours. Stability analysis revealed good thermal tolerance, with the phage maintaining high titers at 4°C, 21°C, 37°C, and 50°C. However, stability significantly declined above 50°C, reaching near complete inactivation at 70°C. The phage was isolated from the gut of *Periplaneta americana* L. The observed optimal stability range (20–50°C) and the primary culture temperature (37°C) suggest mesophilic characteristics, with no evidence of adaptation to extreme temperatures.
