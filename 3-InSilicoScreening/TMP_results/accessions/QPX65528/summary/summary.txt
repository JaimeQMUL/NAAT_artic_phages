- Refrigeration temperatures have the potential to enhance the longevity of bacteriophages on the surfaces of meat products.

Environmental Context:
- The bacteria are associated with the poultry gut, caeca, and environmental reservoirs (litter, dried feces, feed, drinker/feeder lines, etc.).

Concise Summary:
The review notes that the efficacy of bacteriophages is temperature-dependent, showing a reduced effect at ambient temperatures (20 °C) compared to refrigeration temperatures (4 °C). The longevity of phages on food contact surfaces is enhanced at refrigeration temperatures. The target bacteria are zoonotic pathogens commonly found in the poultry gut and persist in the farm environment, including litter, feces, and equipment, suggesting adaptation to mesophilic conditions within these reservoirs.
