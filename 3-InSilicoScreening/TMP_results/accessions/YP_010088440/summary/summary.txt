Paper: PMC12656860.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal thermal stability was reported between 0–40 °C for some phages.
- Other phages showed partial activity at 50 °C, exceeding previously reported levels for some T4-like phages.
- Exposure to temperatures above 60 °C completely inactivated all phage particles.
- The phages maintained viability up to 40 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from sewage treatment plant samples.

Concise Summary:
The phages were characterized for thermal stability, showing optimal stability in the range of 0–40 °C. While some phages retained partial activity up to 50 °C, inactivation was complete above 60 °C. The phages were isolated from sewage treatment plant samples. The study does not explicitly classify the phages as thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic, but the observed stability profile suggests adaptation to mesophilic conditions typical of sewage environments.

The analysis focuses on the temporal changes in the microbial community structure, including the dominance of lactic acid bacteria (LAB) genera such as *Lactococcus*, *Leuconostoc*, and *Pediococcus* over time. The environmental context is the brine derived from fermented cucumbers, which is a mesophilic fermentation system. No specific information regarding thermophilic, psychrophilic, or extreme temperature adaptations was provided for the identified microorganisms.
