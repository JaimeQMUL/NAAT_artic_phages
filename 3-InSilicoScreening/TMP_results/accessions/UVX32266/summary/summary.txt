Thermal Evidence:

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Commensal bacteria from the human nasal and skin microbiomes, and other environmental/clinical isolates.

Concise Summary:
The study focuses on genetic manipulation of commensal bacteria, primarily staphylococci, which are generally mesophiles with an optimal growth temperature of 37°C. The bacteria were isolated from human nasal and skin microbiomes. The methodology relies on a novel technique combining phage transduction with a short, controlled heat shock (e.g., 2 minutes at 48°C–52°C) to overcome restriction-modification (RM) barriers. The heat shock itself is a form of thermal stress applied to the cells, which transiently inactivates DNA-defense systems. While the bacteria are generally mesophilic, the protocol demonstrates the ability to manipulate strains under varying thermal conditions, including the use of 30°C incubation for temperature-sensitive plasmids. The successful application of this method across different species and genera (including *Cutibacterium acnes*, *Bacillus*, and *Listeria*) confirms the utility of temperature-mediated stress to enhance genetic accessibility.

The research utilized genomic data derived from marine environmental samples, including metagenomes and SAGs.
