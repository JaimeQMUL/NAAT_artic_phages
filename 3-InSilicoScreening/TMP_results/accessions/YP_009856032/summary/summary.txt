Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater, rivers, and clinical sites (urine, wounds).

Concise Summary:
Two newly isolated myoviruses were characterized for their lytic activity against multidrug-resistant Enterobacter spp. The optimal growth and lytic activity temperature for both the host bacteria and the phages was determined to be 37 °C. Phage stability studies revealed that the phages retained their initial titers when stored at 4 °C or frozen at -70 °C over a six-month period. Conversely, storage at 37 °C resulted in a complete loss of activity over the same duration. Short-term assays (e.g., adsorption, lytic assays) were conducted at 37 °C. The phages also demonstrated stability when incubated in fresh urine samples at 37 °C for up to 1 hour, suggesting potential for intravesical administration. The phages were isolated from diverse sources, including environmental water samples and clinical specimens, and were classified as belonging to the Tevenvirinae subfamily, with specific genus assignments provided based on genomic analysis.
