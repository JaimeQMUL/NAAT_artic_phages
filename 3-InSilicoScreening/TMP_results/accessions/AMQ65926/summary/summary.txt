- Survival temperature range: Tested at 43 °C, 60 °C, and 80 °C.
- Incubation conditions: Standard incubation was performed at 30 °C or 37 °C.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) and various environmental samples (water, soil, etc.).
- Extreme Environment Isolation: The study utilized samples from WWTPs, suggesting an environmental origin, but no explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic, psychrophilic) was given for the organism's adaptation to extreme temperatures.

Concise Summary:
Two novel virulent phages were isolated from raw sewage samples collected from a wastewater treatment plant. The host bacterium, *Ochrobactrum* spp., was tested across various environmental conditions, with standard growth temperatures noted at 30 °C and 37 °C. Phage stability testing revealed temperature-dependent inactivation: one phage exhibited sensitivity at 43 °C, while the other showed greater resistance but still suffered significant inactivation over time at 43 °C. Survival was assessed up to 80 °C, with one phage showing limited survival at 60 °C. The phages were generally stable across a pH range of 7 to 8, though specific stability differences were noted (one phage preferred higher pH, the other lower pH). The phages were found to be UV-sensitive, with differential resistance observed between the two viral types.
