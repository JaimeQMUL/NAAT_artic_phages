Paper: PMC13193548.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

- No mention of thermophilic adaptation, psychrophilic adaptation, mesophilic growth, heat tolerance, cold tolerance, or temperature-adapted enzymes was found.
- No details regarding membrane adaptations or thermal stress responses were reported.

Environmental Context:
- The study analyzed phosphate transporters across a diverse range of organisms, including bacteria, fungi, algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes, and various flowering plants (including model and cultivated vegetable species).
- The organisms were sourced from various natural and agricultural contexts, but no specific extreme environmental isolation (e.g., hydrothermal vents, deep sea) was detailed for the entire group.

Concise Summary:
The paper provides a comparative analysis of seven types of phosphate transporters across 40 species spanning prokaryotes, fungi, algae, and diverse plant lineages. The focus is on the phylogenetic relationships, structural motifs, and transcriptomic expression patterns of these transporters in response to phosphate availability and tissue type. The analysis reveals that the seven transporter types have distinct evolutionary origins, with PHT3s, pPTs, and SPX-domain PTs being of eukaryotic origin, while PHT1s, PHT2s, PHT4s, and G3Pps are of prokaryotic origin. Key findings include the differential expression patterns of these transporters, particularly the induction of PHT1s and G3Pps under phosphate starvation, and the specialized roles of transporters in root-to-shoot translocation (e.g., PHO1). The study concludes by proposing that PHT1s, G3Pps, and SPX-domain PTs are promising candidates for genetic engineering strategies aimed at developing phosphate-efficient crops, based on their regulated expression patterns in response to nutrient stress.
