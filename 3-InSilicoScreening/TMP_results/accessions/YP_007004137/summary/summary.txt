Environmental Context:
- The microbial communities were analyzed from intratumoral tissues of patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). The source is a human tumor microenvironment, not an extreme natural environment.

Concise Summary:
This study utilized 2bRAD-M sequencing to compare the intratumoral microbiota between TNBC patients achieving pathological complete response (pCR) and those who did not (non-pCR) following neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The analysis identified significant differences in microbial diversity and composition, noting that the non-pCR group exhibited higher diversity and an increased presence of specific gram-negative bacteria (e.g., *Klebsiella*, *Escherichia*, *Acinetobacter*, *Vibrio*, and *Meiothermus*). The study identified seven species capable of distinguishing between the two clinical groups with high predictive accuracy (AUC of 90.3% in the initial cohort). Functional annotation revealed differentially expressed COGs and enriched signaling pathways, suggesting that microbial dysbiosis may correlate with chemotherapy resistance, potentially via LPS-mediated activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. The research establishes the microbiome's potential as a biomarker for predicting NACT efficacy in TNBC, but provides no data regarding the thermal physiology or adaptation of the isolated microbes.
