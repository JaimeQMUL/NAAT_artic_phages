Paper: PMC10054541.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 35 °C (for phage adsorption, one-step growth, and endolysin activity assays).
- Growth temperature range: Assays were conducted at 35 °C, with phage stability tested across a range from 5 °C to 95 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Phage stability was assessed up to 95 °C, with significant loss of titer observed at 65 °C (1000-fold reduction) and complete inactivation at 75 °C and higher.
- Incubation conditions: Endolysin activity was tested at 35 °C for 10 hours (turbidimetry) and thermal stability was tested at 20–70 °C for 1 hour.
- Heat tolerance: Endolysin Gp84 retained activity after incubation at 60 °C, while Gp82 showed significant loss of activity at 60 °C. Gp84 was completely inactivated at 70 °C.
- Cold tolerance: Phage titers remained similar to the control sample incubated at 5 °C.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: No explicit classification (e.g., thermophilic) was given, but the stability data suggests activity maintained up to 60 °C for Gp84.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Isolated from a commercial phage cocktail used in clinical/therapeutic settings.
- Extreme Environment Isolation: No mention of isolation from an extreme environment.

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibited optimal activity and adsorption kinetics at 35 °C. Thermal stability testing showed the phage was highly stable up to 45 °C, with significant inactivation occurring above 65 °C. Endolysin Gp84 demonstrated notable thermal resilience, retaining activity after incubation at 60 °C, whereas Gp82 showed greater thermal sensitivity. The phage maintained viability at 5 °C, indicating cold tolerance. The enzymes were tested for stability across a range up to 70 °C, with Gp84 showing superior stability compared to Gp82 under these conditions. The phage was isolated from a commercial therapeutic phage cocktail, and no evidence of isolation from an extreme environment was reported.

- Stable for 3 months when stored at $4^	ext{o}	ext{C}$.

Environmental Context:

Concise Summary:
The bacteriophage was found to be stable at $4^	ext{o}	ext{C}$ for up to 3 months. It demonstrated thermal stability, maintaining titer after incubation at $70^	ext{o}	ext{C}$ for 60 minutes, but was completely inactivated above $80^	ext{o}	ext{C}$. Optimal storage pH was 7, with viability lost below pH 4 or above pH 11. The phage was isolated from hospital sewage. The data suggest mesophilic stability with good cold tolerance ($4^	ext{o}	ext{C}$) and moderate heat tolerance ($70^	ext{o}	ext{C}$).
