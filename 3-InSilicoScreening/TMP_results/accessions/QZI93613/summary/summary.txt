Concise Summary:
This study provides a comprehensive multi-omic characterization of the gut microbiome, metabolome, and virome in patients with a chronic autoimmune disorder. The analysis focused on taxonomic composition, functional potential, and metabolic crosstalk, revealing significant dysbiosis across bacterial, fungal, and viral kingdoms. Key findings include the enrichment of specific bacterial genera (e.g., *Streptococcus*, *Veillonella*, *Ligilactobacillus*) and fungal species (*Candida*, *Malassezia*) in patients, alongside metabolic remodeling characterized by depletion of protective metabolites (e.g., SCFAs, indole) and enrichment of pro-inflammatory/toxic metabolites. The study utilized *in vitro* assays demonstrating that the dysbiotic microbiota and specific taxa can induce robust proinflammatory cytokine responses (e.g., TNF-$	ext{a}$, IL-6, IL-17) in human PBMCs. Genomic analysis of key associated species revealed abundant virulence factors, immunogenic epitopes, and antimicrobial resistance genes, suggesting molecular mimicry and immune activation as pathogenic mechanisms. The findings establish a multi-kingdom framework linking gut dysbiosis to systemic immune dysregulation, with diagnostic signatures validated in independent cohorts. The analysis did not yield specific data regarding the thermal adaptation or growth temperature profiles of the identified taxa.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated antibiotic resistance gene transfer in *E. coli* strains isolated from raw milk and dairy farm environments under heavy metal stress. The research focused on the molecular mechanisms of co-resistance, specifically demonstrating that low-to-moderate $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ concentrations enhanced the conjugative transfer frequency between strains via ROS-dependent pathways. The physiological analysis provided no data regarding the optimal, range, or survival temperatures, nor did it discuss any specific thermal adaptations (e.g., thermophilic or psychrophilic). The organisms were sourced from agricultural/dairy environments.
