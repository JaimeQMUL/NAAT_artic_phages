Concise Summary:
The study investigated the transfer of antibiotic resistance genes in $\text{E. coli}$ strains isolated from raw milk and dairy farm environments under heavy metal stress. The research focused on the molecular mechanisms of co-resistance, specifically demonstrating that low-to-moderate $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ concentrations (1-10 $\mu\text{g/L}$) significantly enhanced intrageneric conjugative transfer frequency, suggesting $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ promotes gene transfer via ROS-dependent pathways. The analysis detailed the role of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ in increasing cell aggregation, decreasing negative surface charge, and improving membrane permeability, alongside modulating stress responses like the SOS response. No data regarding the optimal, growth, or survival temperatures, or any specific thermal adaptations (e.g., mesophilic, thermophilic) of the isolated strains were presented.
