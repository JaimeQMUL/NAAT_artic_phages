Paper: 42176583.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- No specific optimal, range, or survival temperatures were provided.
- Heat stimulation (Hs) and Microwave stimulation (Ws) were used as treatments, indicating thermal application was a key variable.

Environmental Context:
- The larvae were reared on chicken manure, which serves as the environmental source/habitat.

Concise Summary:
The study investigated the enhancement of antibacterial efficacy in larvae reared on chicken manure when subjected to pathogen challenge and subsequent thermal treatments. While specific optimal or survival temperatures were not detailed, the experimental design utilized both heat-stimulated (Hs) and microwave-stimulated (Ws) conditions, suggesting thermal stress was a critical variable for inducing antimicrobial protein expression (defensin and cecropin). The application of heat and microwave energy significantly upregulated these protective mechanisms, leading to enhanced antibacterial activity, particularly against one tested pathogen. The optimal retention of anti-pathogen activity was achieved following pretreatment and subsequent drying, suggesting desiccation tolerance was also a factor in preserving efficacy.
