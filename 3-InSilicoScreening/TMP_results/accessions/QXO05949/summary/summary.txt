Concise Summary:
The study characterized the prevalence, antibiotic susceptibility, and genotyping of specific bacterial genera isolated from wild bird faecal samples. The analysis focused on molecular and antimicrobial resistance profiles, reporting a prevalence of one genus at 9.2% and another at 0.2%. Resistance patterns were noted, with the highest resistance observed against amoxycillin-clavulanic acid (30.2%) and tetracycline (15.1%) in one of the recovered genera, alongside a multidrug resistance rate of 7.5%. Genetic diversity was assessed via ERIC-PCR, yielding 21 distinct forms for one genus. The authors concluded that the detection of enteropathogenic, antibiotic-resistant, and genetically diverse bacteria suggests a potential health risk associated with the environmental source.
