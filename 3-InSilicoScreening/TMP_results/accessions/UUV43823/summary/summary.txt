Paper: PMC11946847.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal pH values for phage Lydia ranged from 7 to 10.
- Phage stability was assessed across a range of temperatures: −20 °C, +4 °C, +28 °C, +37 °C, +50 °C, and +60 °C for 1 h.
- Titer remained relatively stable between −20 °C and +60 °C.
- A complete loss of phage viability was observed after 1 h of incubation at +70 °C.
- The phage was found to be stable at +4 °C and +37 °C (optimal pH range).
- The phage was found to be unstable at −20 °C (50.8% titer decrease) and +60 °C (53.7% titer decrease).
- The phage was found to be stable at +37 °C for adsorption and one-step growth experiments.

Environmental Context:
- The phage was isolated from a river water sample (Selenga River).
- The phage was characterized as belonging to a family known for phages infecting various bacteria, including those from diverse sources like water and soil.

Concise Summary:
The phage exhibited optimal stability and activity at mesophilic conditions, with optimal pH ranging from 7 to 10. Its stability was tested across a broad temperature gradient, showing relative stability between −20 °C and +60 °C, while complete inactivation occurred at +70 °C. The phage was successfully propagated and studied at 37 °C, which was used for adsorption and one-step growth assays. The phage was isolated from a river water source, indicating an environmental origin. The analysis did not explicitly classify the phage as thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic, but the operational stability range suggests mesophilic adaptation, with clear thermal limitations above 60 °C.
