Paper: PMC9389114.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37°C (for bacterial growth in LB medium).
- Growth temperature range: Tested stability between 30°C and 60°C for 60 minutes, with high stability (>86% retention) observed at 60°C. No viable phages were detected at temperatures $\ge$ 70°C.
- Survival temperature range: Stable between 30°C and 60°C for 60 minutes. The phage retained infectivity at ambient temperatures (implied by stability testing).
- Incubation conditions: Bacterial growth was monitored at 4°C (refrigeration temperature) and 25°C (room temperature) in food matrices. In liquid culture, bacterial counts were significantly reduced at 4°C, and regrowth was not observed throughout the incubation period. At 25°C, bacterial regrowth was observed.
- Environmental source/habitat: Isolated from sewage of a commercial pig farm.
- Adaptation/Classification: The phage demonstrated stability at temperatures up to 60°C, suggesting good thermal tolerance within the mesophilic range, but no explicit classification (e.g., psychrotolerant, mesophile) was provided.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage from a commercial pig farm.

Concise Summary:
The phage was isolated from sewage of a commercial pig farm. Its optimal growth temperature for the bacterial host was 37°C. The phage exhibited significant thermal stability, retaining over 86% infectivity after 60 minutes incubation at 60°C, but showed no viability at $\ge$ 70°C. In food matrices, the phage demonstrated strong bactericidal activity at refrigeration temperatures (4°C), where bacterial regrowth was not observed over extended periods (up to 7 days in milk). At room temperature (25°C), bacterial regrowth was observed, indicating that lower temperatures are more favorable for phage efficacy. The phage's stability profile suggests suitability for food preservation processes involving refrigeration.

Concise Summary:

The data indicates that phages are stable at 37°C, which is relevant for mammalian applications. Optimal survival was noted in the pH range of 5–8, consistent with the physiological conditions of the rumen and bovine blood. Stability was poor at pH levels below 3. The phages were sourced from diverse environmental habitats, including sewage, feces, and various animal products. No explicit classification regarding thermophilic, psychrophilic, or mesophilic adaptation was provided for the phages; however, the stability data suggests suitability for mesophilic conditions relevant to mammalian hosts.
