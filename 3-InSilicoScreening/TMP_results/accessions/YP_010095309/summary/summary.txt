Concise Summary:
Glycine betaine (GB) is a crucial compatible solute that enhances the resistance of both plants and microorganisms to various abiotic stresses, including high temperatures and cold. At high temperatures, GB helps maintain cell membrane fluidity and prevents protein misfolding by acting as a molecular chaperone, thereby stabilizing cellular functions and energy metabolism. Similarly, GB contributes to cold tolerance by helping to maintain membrane integrity. The research heavily focuses on metabolic engineering to enhance GB production in microorganisms, particularly in chassis strains derived from extreme environments (e.g., halophiles). While specific optimal growth temperatures are not provided, the context repeatedly emphasizes the need for enhanced stress resistance, suggesting the engineered systems aim for robust performance across challenging environmental gradients. The mechanisms discussed—membrane stabilization, chaperone activity, and osmotic regulation—are direct responses to thermal stress.
