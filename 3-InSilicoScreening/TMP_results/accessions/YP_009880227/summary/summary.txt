Concise Summary:
The phages were characterized using assays incubated at 28°C, which is the optimal temperature for bacterial growth and phage activity. The phages demonstrated viability after 24 hours at 4°C and 25°C, suggesting cold tolerance relevant to the natural environment of the pathogen. Thermal stability assays showed that infectivity was maintained at 37°C for one phage, but significantly reduced for the others. Activity was minimal or undetectable at 50°C and higher. The phages exhibited stability across a wide pH range (pH 3–11), with the most extreme pH values (pH 1 and pH 13) eliminating detectable infectivity. The authors noted that the natural development conditions for the associated pathogen are cool and wet. The data suggest a general mesophilic nature with specific adaptations to cooler temperatures, and the observed stability profiles indicate differential thermal resilience among the three distinct phage lineages.
