Paper: PMC4529890.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Phages retained 100% infection activity after a 1 min incubation at 90°C, 100°C, and 121°C.
- Phages were stable after 180 min at -80°C, -20°C, 4°C, 23°C, 30°C, 37°C, and 50°C.
- Specific phage stability noted: SIOΦ lost total activity after 2 min at 80°C, 30 min at 70°C, and showed reduced activity at 60°C after 3 h. SUBω lost infectivity after 1 min at 80°C, 5 min at 70°C, and 180 min at 60°C. SPOσ was inactive after 2 min at 80°C, 15 min at 70°C, and 60 min at 60°C. ARπ lost activity after 2 min at 80°C, 10 min at 70°C, and 3 hours at 60°C.
- General observation: Members of Myoviridae and Siphoviridae families are considered resistant to large temperature fluctuations.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from soil.

Concise Summary:
Four bacteriophages infecting *Bacillus subtilis* were characterized. Thermal stability testing demonstrated high resistance, with all phages retaining activity after 1 min at 90°C, 100°C, and 121°C. Phages maintained activity across a broad range, including stability at -80°C up to 50°C. Specific phage stability varied: SIOΦ, SUBω, and SPOσ showed differential inactivation rates at elevated temperatures (e.g., SIOΦ activity loss at 60°C after 3h; SUBω loss at 180 min at 60°C). The phages were isolated from soil, and the general classification suggests resistance to large temperature fluctuations, indicating robust thermal tolerance suitable for industrial applications.
