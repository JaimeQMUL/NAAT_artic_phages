Paper: PMC12390700.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

- Siphophage reproduction: Effective at temperature $\le$ 25 °C.

- Giant phage vB_AerVM_332-Vera infection: 5–10 °C.
- Phage 332-Yuliya lytic activity: Decreased at 5 °C, low activity at 5 °C, and inactive at 37 °C.
- Phage 332-Vera reproduction: Weakly multiplied at low temperatures (5 °C and 10 °C).
- Phage 332-Igor reproduction: Narrower temperature range, with an optimum at 25 °C.
- Real-time PCR results: Phage 332-Yuliya propagated efficiently at 10 °C to 25 °C; low efficiency at 5 °C; traces observed at 37 °C.
- Real-time PCR results: Phage 332-Igor optimum growth at 25 °C.
- Real-time PCR results: Phage 332-Vera showed activity across tested temperatures, but the text implies different optimal ranges for the three phages.

Environmental Context:
- Source: Water + sediments sample collected in a freshwater pond.
- Habitat: Freshwater pond environment, suggesting exposure to variable seasonal temperatures (e.g., 4 °C in winter, above 30 °C in summer).

Concise Summary:
Three novel phages (one siphophage and two giant phages) were isolated from a freshwater pond water + sediment sample, infecting a host bacterium capable of growing across a wide temperature range (5 °C to 37 °C, optimum 25–37 °C). The phages exhibit distinct thermal preferences: the siphophage is effective at $\le$ 25 °C, the phage vB_AerVM_332-Igor has an optimum at 25 °C, and vB_AerVM_332-Vera infects at 5–10 °C. In vitro kinetic assays confirmed these differences, showing the siphophage thriving between 10 °C and 25 °C, while the giant phages showed narrower optimal ranges. The environmental context suggests the phages are adapted to fluctuating temperatures characteristic of natural freshwater bodies, allowing them to exploit the host across different seasons. No explicit classification (e.g., psychrophilic, mesophilic) was assigned, but the observed activity profiles suggest adaptation to mesophilic conditions with distinct temperature niches for each phage.
