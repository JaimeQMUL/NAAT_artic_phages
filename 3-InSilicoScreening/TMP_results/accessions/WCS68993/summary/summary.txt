Concise Summary:
The paper compares two distinct modes of spore dormancy: 'dry dormancy' and 'wet dormancy,' using a dynamical systems framework. The comparison focuses on the physical mechanisms of life suspension rather than specific thermal physiology. Dry dormancy involves immobilization via dehydration and material constraints, arresting global biological time by suppressing molecular diffusion. Wet dormancy occurs in hydrated spores, maintaining molecular mobility and slow irreversible processes (like gene expression), but global progression (growth/division) is arrested because local activity fails to propagate. The authors classify these states based on a physical design space defined by hydration, molecular mobility, energetic flux, and cross-scale coupling, rather than providing explicit thermal classifications for the organisms studied.
