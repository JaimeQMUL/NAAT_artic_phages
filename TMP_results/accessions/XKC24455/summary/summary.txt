Paper: PMC12975815.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37°C
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated, but assays were conducted at 37°C.
- Survival temperature range: Maintained viability at 37°C to 42°C for up to 24h. Complete abolition of viability observed at -20°C and 60°C within the first hour.
- Incubation conditions: Assays were conducted at 37°C for various periods (e.g., 24h for pH/chloroform stability).
- Environmental source/habitat: Sewage-contaminated stream water.
- Thermophilic adaptation: No evidence of thermophilic adaptation.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: No evidence of psychrophilic adaptation.
- Mesophilic growth: The phage maintained activity at 37°C, consistent with mesophilic conditions.
- Heat tolerance: Showed reduced viability after 4h at 50°C (nearly two-log reduction), but remained viable at 37°C-42°C for 24h.
- Cold tolerance: Viability was completely abolished at -20°C within the first hour.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: No specific mention of temperature-adapted enzymes.
- Membrane adaptations: Not discussed.
- Thermal stress responses: The phage demonstrated stability across moderate temperature variations (37°C–42°C) for up to 24h.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Sewage-contaminated stream water.

Concise Summary:
The phage demonstrated optimal activity and stability at 37°C, consistent with mesophilic characteristics. It maintained viability across a moderate temperature range of 37°C to 42°C for up to 24 hours. The phage exhibited poor cold tolerance, with viability completely lost at -20°C within one hour. Heat tolerance was tested, showing a significant reduction in viability after 4 hours at 50°C, but remained stable at temperatures up to 42°C for 24 hours. The phage was isolated from sewage-contaminated stream water, indicating an environmental origin. The stability profile suggests suitability for moderate environmental and potential clinical applications, while the complete loss of viability at extreme temperatures (e.g., 60°C, -20°C) defines its operational thermal limits.
