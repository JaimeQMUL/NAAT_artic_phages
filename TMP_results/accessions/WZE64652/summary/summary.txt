Thermal Evidence:
- The study investigated 106 thermophilic eubacteria and 21 thermophilic archaea.
- Specific temperature ranges mentioned for isolated organisms include: (70-<100) °C, 80 °C, 113 °C, 110 °C, 75 °C, (70–100) °C, 60 °C, 80 °C, 70–100 °C, and 100 °C.
- The general context is the study of organisms thriving at high temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- Habitats/Sources include deep-sea hydrothermal vents, decaying vegetables, and general high-temperature environments.

Concise Summary:
This research focused on quorum sensing mechanisms in thermophilic bacteria and archaea. The organisms analyzed were sourced from high-temperature environments, with reported growth/survival temperatures spanning from 60 °C up to 113 °C. The study explicitly addresses the adaptation of these microbes to high temperatures, noting that the investigation was motivated by the known biofilm formation capabilities of thermophiles. The analysis of quorum sensing systems (AI-1, peptide, AI-2) was conducted across a diverse group of thermophiles, confirming the relevance of AI-2 as a stable signaling molecule in hot environments, while AI-1 was deemed heat labile. The findings suggest that the primary mode of communication investigated in these thermophiles is related to the AI-2 system, involving the presence of LuxS, Pfs, LsrB, and RbsB homologs, which are crucial for signal synthesis and detection under thermophilic conditions. The study highlights the metabolic adaptations, such as the detoxification of S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH) via SAH hydrolase, which is highly conserved across many thermophiles, suggesting a general adaptation to high-temperature metabolic stress beyond just quorum sensing.
