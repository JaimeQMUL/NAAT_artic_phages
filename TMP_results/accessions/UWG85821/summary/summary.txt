Concise Summary:
This paper is a biomechanical finite-element analysis concerning the torsional stability of fixation constructs for Pauwels type III femoral neck fractures. It compares three fixation methods: multiple screw fixation, a dynamic hip screw (DHS) with an antirotation screw, and the femoral neck system (FNS). The analysis simulated muscle forces during the leg-swing phase to assess stability under rotational loading. Key findings indicate that the FNS provides rotational stability comparable to multiple screw fixation and reduces stress at the fracture site, making it the preferred option over the DHS, which showed the highest equivalent von Mises stress, greatest fragment displacement, and highest strain energy density (SED) in the cancellous bone. The study focuses entirely on orthopedic biomechanics and does not contain any information regarding microbial physiology, temperature, or growth conditions.
