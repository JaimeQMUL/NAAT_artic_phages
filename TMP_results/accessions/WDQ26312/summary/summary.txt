Concise Summary:
The phage exhibited optimal growth and lytic activity at 37 °C. Its stability was demonstrated across a wide temperature range, maintaining integrity from -80 °C up to 37 °C. However, significant declines in viability and lytic activity were observed at temperatures exceeding 50 °C. The phage was isolated from clinically relevant Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) strains, suggesting a mesophilic lifestyle adapted to human body temperature. No specific adaptations for thermophilic or psychrophilic environments were reported.

Key thermal evidence relates to the activating enzymes: one RNase was characterized as heat-tolerant, while the other was noted for its acid tolerance. The functional assays utilized pre-heating of bacteria to achieve high activity, demonstrating condition-dependent responsiveness rather than specifying the optimal growth or survival temperature range for the source organisms. The bacteria were tested in a clinical setting (HAP).
