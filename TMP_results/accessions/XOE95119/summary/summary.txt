Concise Summary:
The study investigated viral assemblages in a hypersaline estuary subjected to various pulse disturbances, including freshwater inundation and freeze events. Temperature was a key variable, with observed water temperatures ranging from 17.7°C to 30.8°C. The analysis specifically noted that viral functional potential was significantly impacted by extreme cold weather events. Viruses associated with low temperatures exhibited an enrichment of AMGs related to oxidative phosphorylation and sulfur cycling. The overall environment is a hypersaline estuary that experiences seasonal temperature fluctuations and acute thermal stress events (freeze events). The findings suggest that the viral community's functional response to temperature extremes differs from its response to salinity changes, indicating a functional resilience or adaptation to cold stress within this high-salinity system.
