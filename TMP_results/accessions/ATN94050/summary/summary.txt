Thermal Evidence:

- Thermal stress responses: The engineered strain exhibited a pronounced cold-sensitive phenotype, showing substantial decrease in viability at 30 °C and near abolition of growth at 20 °C, suggesting impaired ribosome function under sub-optimal temperatures.

Environmental Context:
- The organism was not explicitly stated to be isolated from an extreme environment.
- Explicit classification: The study utilized a surrogate model for a pathogenic species, and the growth characteristics suggest mesophilic growth, with temperature sensitivity noted at lower ranges.

Concise Summary:
The engineered strain demonstrated optimal growth kinetics at 37 °C. Temperature-dependent assays revealed a significant cold-sensitive phenotype, with growth impairment becoming pronounced at 30 °C and nearly abolished at 20 °C. The organism was cultured under standard laboratory conditions, with no evidence of isolation from extreme thermal environments. The observed temperature sensitivity is linked to defects in ribosome assembly or maturation, which are compromised at non-optimal temperatures.
