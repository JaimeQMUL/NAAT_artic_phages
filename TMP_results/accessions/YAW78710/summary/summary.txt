Paper: PMC10327644.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Warmer water temperatures are associated with a shift in the dominant taxa, specifically an increase in *Vibrio*, *Allivibrio*, and *Photobacterium*.
- Cold water temperatures are associated with the dominance of *Leuconostoc* and *Weissella*.
- The influence of temperature on the microbiota is noted, with specific shifts observed in response to temperature changes.

Environmental Context:
- Marine cage aquaculture systems (open environment).

Concise Summary:
The microbial community composition in the gut of a cultured finfish species is shown to be temperature-dependent. Specifically, the microbiota shifts from being dominated by *Leuconostoc* and *Weissella* in cold water conditions to being dominated by *Vibrio*, *Allivibrio*, and *Photobacterium* when water temperatures increase. This indicates a direct correlation between ambient temperature and the composition of the resident microbial community in this aquaculture setting.


Paper: PMC12145637.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal infectivity observed between 7–8 pH units (pH stability).
- Maintained high stability between 4°C and 30°C (Temperature stability).
- Progressive decline in viability observed between 40°C and 50°C (Temperature stability).
- Complete inactivation occurred above 60°C (Temperature stability).
- The phages maintained lytic efficiency across a broad pH range (5–9) (pH stability).

Environmental Context:
- Yellow Sea coastal waters (Source of isolation).

Concise Summary:
The isolated phages demonstrated robust thermal stability, maintaining high lytic efficiency across a temperature range of 4°C to 30°C. Viability showed a progressive decline between 40°C and 50°C, with complete inactivation noted above 60°C. The phages exhibited broad pH tolerance, remaining active between pH 5 and pH 9, with optimal infectivity centered around pH 7–8. These stability profiles suggest potential for application in aquaculture settings. The phages were isolated from Yellow Sea coastal waters.
