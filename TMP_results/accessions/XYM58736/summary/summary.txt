Concise Summary:

Its protective mechanisms involve stabilizing cell membranes, preventing lipid peroxidation, and acting as a molecular chaperone to mitigate protein misfolding at elevated temperatures. In microbial systems, the exogenous application of GB during fermentation enhances process efficiency and reduces temperature-related inhibition. While the paper reviews the biosynthesis pathways in various microorganisms, it focuses more on the *effect* of GB on thermal stability rather than providing specific optimal growth temperatures for the enzymes or organisms. The enzymes and organisms discussed are frequently sourced from extreme environments, such as halophiles, suggesting inherent adaptations to harsh physicochemical conditions, which often include temperature fluctuations.
