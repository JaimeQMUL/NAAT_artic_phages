Paper: PMC13128607.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

Environmental Context:
- The study analyzed pathogens and microecology from the pediatric lower respiratory tract (LRTIs) via throat swab samples. The identified organisms are associated with the human upper and lower respiratory tract environment.

Concise Summary:
This study focused on the pathogen profile and microbial ecology of pediatric lower respiratory tract infections using targeted next-generation sequencing of throat swab samples. The analysis identified key pathogens, including *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, and correlated pathogen detection, relative abundance (RA), and overall microbial diversity (Shannon/Simpson indices) with the severity of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP). The findings highlight that *M. pneumoniae* was a major driver of severe CAP (SCAP) during the study period. Furthermore, the research established an ecological dimension, showing that reduced upper respiratory microbial diversity and shifts in commensal abundances were associated with pneumonia severity. The study utilized non-invasive throat swab tNGS as a valuable screening tool, though it noted that BALF remains superior for definitive lower respiratory pathogen identification in severe cases. No information regarding the thermal physiology, optimal growth temperature, or thermal adaptation of the detected microorganisms was provided.
