Environmental Context:
- Freshwater aquatic system.
- The study involved laboratory incubation conditions using various dissolved organic matter (DOM) sources (algae-derived and soil-derived).

Concise Summary:
This study investigated the inhibition of methylmercury (MeHg) uptake by three freshwater phytoplankton species using a laboratory system. The research focused on how the source of dissolved organic matter (DOM)—specifically comparing algae-derived versus soil-derived DOM—affects MeHg bioconcentration. A key finding was that algae-derived DOM significantly inhibited cellular MeHg bioconcentration by 77-93%, whereas soil-derived DOM showed little effect. Characterization revealed that the molecular size of sulfur-containing compounds, rather than thiol concentration, was critical for regulating MeHg uptake. The authors noted that future changes in plankton growth due to climate change and nutrient loading could alter DOM composition, thereby impacting aquatic Hg cycling and associated risks to human and wild life. No data regarding the thermal physiology or temperature optima of the tested organisms were presented.

Environmental Context:
- The study utilized microbial consortia from a natural meso-eutrophic river.

Concise Summary:
Microbial degradation of dissolved organic matter (DOM) derived from algal sources was studied over a 28-day incubation period using consortia from a natural meso-eutrophic river. The research focused on the impact of this degradation on methylmercury (MeHg) uptake by a widespread phytoplankton species. Key findings include significant degradation of dissolved organic carbon (64.3–74.1%) and changes in DOM composition, shifting it towards a more humic-like profile. Crucially, the study demonstrated that DOM remaining after microbial degradation still reduced MeHg uptake by the phytoplankton by 32.7–52.7% compared to controls. The authors conclude that microbial degradation of DOM may inhibit MeHg uptake, suggesting that the potential role of microbes in altering aquatic Hg cycling should be integrated into future risk assessments. No specific thermal parameters were provided for the organisms or processes studied.
