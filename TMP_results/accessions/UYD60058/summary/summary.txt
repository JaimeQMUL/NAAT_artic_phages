Paper: PMC13029702.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 37 °C (Control for bacterial growth and phage assays).
- Growth temperature range: Not explicitly stated for the phages, but bacterial growth was monitored at 37 °C.
- Survival temperature range: The encapsulated phages maintained viability at 4 °C, 21 °C, and even showed detectable titres at 80 °C (10 min) compared to unencapsulated phages.
- Incubation conditions: Assays were conducted at 37 °C for bacterial growth and phage assays. Long-term storage was tested at 4 °C and 21 °C.
- Environmental source/habitat: Wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) and wastewater samples.
- Thermophilic adaptation: No mention of thermophilic adaptation.
- Psychrophilic adaptation: No mention of psychrophilic adaptation.
- Mesophilic growth: The bacterial hosts were incubated at 37 °C, indicating mesophilic growth conditions for the bacterial component.
- Heat tolerance: Encapsulation significantly enhanced phage survival at elevated temperatures, with detectable titres maintained at 80 °C (10 min) for encapsulated phages, compared to unencapsulated phages.
- Cold tolerance: Both free and encapsulated phages maintained high viability at 4 °C over 3 months.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: No specific mention of temperature-adapted enzymes.
- Membrane adaptations: No specific mention of membrane adaptations.
- Thermal stress responses: Encapsulation provided protection against thermal stress, particularly at elevated temperatures (up to 80 °C).

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) and wastewater samples.

Concise Summary:
Bacteriophages were isolated from wastewater treatment plants, indicating an environmental source. The bacterial hosts were maintained under mesophilic conditions (optimal growth at 37 °C). The study extensively tested thermal stability, demonstrating that encapsulation significantly enhanced phage survival across various temperatures. Specifically, encapsulated phages retained detectable viability at elevated temperatures, including 80 °C (10 min), where unencapsulated phages showed severe inactivation. Furthermore, the phages exhibited robust cold tolerance, maintaining high viability over 3 months at 4 °C. The protective effect of the alginate matrix was crucial in mitigating thermal stress, suggesting enhanced thermal resilience for the formulated product compared to the free phage particles.
