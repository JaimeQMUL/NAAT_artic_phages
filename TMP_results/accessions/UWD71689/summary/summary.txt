Paper: PMC13175151.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- No temperature-related data was found in the provided text.

Environmental Context:

Concise Summary:
This paper is a biomechanical finite-element analysis concerning the torsional stability of fixation constructs for Pauwels type III femoral neck fractures. It compares three fixation methods: multiple screw fixation, a dynamic hip screw (DHS) with an antirotation screw, and a femoral neck system (FNS). The analysis simulated muscle forces during the leg-swing phase to assess stability under rotational loading. Key findings indicate that the FNS provides rotational stability comparable to multiple screw fixation and is preferred over the DHS construct due to superior performance under simulated hip rotation. The DHS construct showed the highest equivalent von Mises stress, greatest fragment displacement, and highest strain energy density, suggesting lower stability during rotation. The FNS was concluded to be the preferred option due to its combination of rotational stability and compressive strength.

No information regarding microbial physiology, temperature, or growth conditions was present in the article.
