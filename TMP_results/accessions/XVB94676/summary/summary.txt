Paper: PMC13061930.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal replication/activity observed at 28°C (for isolation/assay incubation).
- Viability maintained after 24h incubation at 4°C and 25°C.
- Infectivity retained at 37°C for one phage, but reduced for the other two.
- Infectivity was minimal or undetectable at 50°C and above.
- The phages showed viability across a broad pH range (pH 3–11), with elimination at pH 1 and pH 13.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from sewage and soil samples.

Concise Summary:
The phages were characterized using incubation and assay temperatures centered around 28°C. They demonstrated viability at cooler temperatures (4°C and 25°C), which aligns with the cold spring conditions typical of the pathogen's outbreak period. While one phage retained infectivity at 37°C, activity significantly decreased at higher temperatures (≥50°C). The stability profile suggests a preference for mesophilic conditions, with the observed tolerance at 4°C indicating psychrotolerance, and the loss of activity at 50°C+ suggesting a lack of thermophilic adaptation. The phages were isolated from environmental sources (sewage and soil) and showed stability across a wide pH range, suggesting general environmental resilience, though extreme pH levels were inhibitory.

The phages are highly sensitive to extreme pH, remaining stable between pH 4.0 and 7.0, while being inactivated at pH 1.0 and 2.0. The phages were isolated from wastewater originating from animal husbandry and hospitals. The data suggest mesophilic characteristics with limited thermal tolerance at elevated temperatures.
