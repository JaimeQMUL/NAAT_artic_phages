Concise Summary:
This study focuses on the structural basis of lipopolysaccharide O-antigen chain length modulation in a gram-negative bacterium using cryo-electron microscopy. The research details the structure and dynamics of the copolymerase component responsible for this modulation. The mechanism proposed is that the O-antigen chain length is determined by the internal volume of the copolymerase's periplasmic domain, which acts as a scaffold for polymerization. The analysis compares the structures of the copolymerase from the studied bacterium with a related homolog, revealing structural differences that lead to a significantly larger internal volume in the studied complex. The study also investigates the complex's dynamic behavior, showing conformational changes like 'ratcheting' and 'scissoring' during opening, which is hypothesized to facilitate the release of the completed polysaccharide chain. The findings suggest that the entire internal periplasmic volume, rather than a single binding site, controls the final chain length. No data regarding the organism's optimal growth temperature, temperature range, or thermal adaptations were presented.
