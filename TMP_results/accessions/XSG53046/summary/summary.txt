Paper: PMC12005167.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:

- Cold tolerance: Demonstrated ability to respond transcriptionally to cold exposure, particularly in aposymbiotic states.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: Intertidal zone, ranging from southern Massachusetts to the Gulf of Mexico.
- Extreme Environment Isolation: The species is noted for its hardiness and ability to cope with extreme shifts in salinity and temperature, including exposure to near-freezing temperatures in the intertidal zone.

Concise Summary:
The organism exhibits remarkable thermal plasticity, tolerating a broad range from near freezing up to 24°C. Its survival strategy includes entering a dormancy-like state (winter quiescence) when temperatures drop below 6°C, suggesting significant psychrophilic adaptation. Comparative genomic analysis highlighted gene family expansions in the host related to sleep promotion, feeding suppression, and circadian rhythms, which are hypothesized to underpin this cold-adaptation mechanism. While the symbiont showed a stronger response to chronic heat stress than the host, the host's ability to survive freezing temperatures is a key adaptive trait linked to its temperate, intertidal habitat.
