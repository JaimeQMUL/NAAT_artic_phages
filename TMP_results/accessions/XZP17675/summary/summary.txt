Paper: PMC13061930.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal replication/activity observed at 28°C (for isolation/assay incubation).
- Viability maintained after 24h incubation at 4°C and 25°C.
- Infectivity retained at 37°C for one phage, but reduced for the other two.
- Infectivity was minimal or undetectable at 50°C and above.
- The phages showed viability across a broad pH range (pH 3–11), with elimination at pH 1 and pH 13.

Environmental Context:
- Isolated from sewage and soil samples.

Concise Summary:
The phages were characterized under laboratory conditions, with optimal replication and assay incubation temperatures set at 28°C. The phages demonstrated significant stability at cooler temperatures, retaining viability after 24 hours at 4°C and 25°C, which aligns with the cold spring conditions typical of the pathogen's outbreak period. Thermal stability testing showed that while one phage maintained infectivity at 37°C, the other two showed marked reductions at this temperature. Infectivity was lost at 50°C and above. The phages exhibited broad pH tolerance (pH 3–11), suggesting resilience across various environmental pH fluctuations. The phages were isolated from sewage and soil, indicating an environmental reservoir. The data suggest a general adaptation to mesophilic conditions, with specific evidence pointing towards psychrotolerance given the observed stability at 4°C and 25°C, which is relevant to the pathogen's natural environment.
