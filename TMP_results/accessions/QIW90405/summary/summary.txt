Concise Summary:
The provided paper details the development and enhancement of the 'treestructure' R package, a statistical tool based on coalescent theory designed to detect unobserved population structure within time-scaled phylogenetic trees. The methodology involves testing subclades for deviations in their Time to the Most Recent Common Ancestor (TMRCA) distributions. Key enhancements include automatic selection of clustering hyperparameters using the Caliński–Harabasz index, the ability to filter clusters based on branch support values (e.g., bootstrap/posterior probability) to ensure phylogenetic confidence, and a function to update cluster assignments online with new samples. The analysis is purely computational and phylogenetic, focusing on population genetics and evolutionary dynamics, and contains no physiological data regarding temperature adaptation, growth conditions, or thermal tolerance for any associated microorganisms.
