Abstract

Objectives

Carbapenemase-producing Gram-negative bacteria represent a major clinical challenge. Rapid detection and characterization of carbapenemase group is essential for guiding therapy and for infection control. We evaluated the performance of Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases, a novel lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA), for detecting the main carbapenemase groups from bacterial culture.

Materials and methods

A multicentre collection of 351 well-characterized clinical Gram-negative isolates, studied by whole-genome sequencing, was tested using this assay. 57 KPC, 53 NDM, 63 VIM, 68 OXA-48-like, 64 IMP and 100 carbapenem resistant non-carbapenemase producers were included.

Results

The LFIA showed an overall sensitivity of 96.4% and specificity of 100%. The positive predictive value (PPV) was 100%, and the negative predictive value (NPV) was 91.7%. No false-positives or cross-reactions with extended-spectrum β-lactamases (ESBLs) or cephalosporinases were observed either with isolates harbouring multiple carbapenemases. Detection rates for IMP and NDM variants reached 100%. Sensitivity for KPC, OXA-like and VIM was slightly lower (93.0%, 95.6% and 96.8%, respectively). Nine false-negative results were observed: KPC-31 (n = 4), VIM-63 (n = 2) and OXA-1054-producing (n = 3) isolates. Increasing the bacterial inoculum managed to obtain a positive result for KPC-31 and VIM-63, but OXA-1054 remained undetected.

Conclusions

Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases is a rapid, accurate, and reliable tool for detecting carbapenemases in clinical settings. While some rare variants may escape detection, its strong performance across diverse bacterial species supports its use as part of routine diagnostics to guide timely antimicrobial therapy and control the spread of resistance.

Introduction

The global emergence of carbapenemase-producing bacteria requires rapid identification and differentiation of carbapenemase types to ensure effective treatment and limit the resistance dissemination. The introduction of novel β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combinations, with specific carbapenemase group spectrum, reinforces the need for rapid carbapenemase group identification.1 Several genotypic and phenotypic methods have been developed for carbapenemase detection and characterization, varying their performance depending on bacterial species and the enzyme.2 Although molecular tests such as PCR/sequencing remain the gold standard, they are costly, require specialized expertise and equipment,3,4 and may miss emerging or non-targeted variants.2 Rapid phenotypic assays (e.g. RAPIDEC CARBA NP or β-CARBA)5 detect carbapenem hydrolysis but do not identify specific enzymes.2,4 As an alternative, lateral flow immunochromatographic assays (LFIA), antibody-based membrane tests that detect specific carbapenemase enzymes through antigen–antibody interactions, allow rapid identification directly from cultured isolates and clinical samples.4,6–8 The most commercialized LFIAs are K-SeT R.E.S.I.S.T and NG-Test CARBA-5, targeting the five major carbapenemase groups (KPC, NDM, VIM, IMP and OXA-48-like). However, false-negative and false-positive results have been reported for certain variants and bacterial species.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the performance of a novel LFIA, Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases, for detecting the five main carbapenemase groups using well-characterized whole-genome sequenced (WGS) Gram-negative isolates.

Materials and methods

Strains collection

A total of 351 WGS Gram-negative bacilli (2017–2025) were included: 100 non-carbapenemase producers (excluding Morganellaceae) resistant to at least one carbapenem, and 251 carbapenemase-producing isolates. Bacterial identification was performed by MALDI-TOF and ribosomal multilocus sequence typing (MLST).

Carbapenemase genes characterization

All isolates underwent antimicrobial susceptibility testing using the Microscan NMDR1 panel (Beckman Coulter), and carbapenem hydrolysis was assessed using the β-CARBA test. All isolates were also tested with NG-Test CARBA-5 following the manufacturer’s instructions. Genomic DNA was extracted (DNeasy Blood and Tissue Kit. Qiagen), libraries were prepared (Nextera DNA Library Prep Kit. Illumina) and sequenced (Illumina MiSeq). Reads were trimmed and de novo assembled using CLC Genomics Workbench, v.9.5.2. Genome quality metrics are shown (Table S1, available as Supplementary data at JAC Online). MLST was performed using MLSTFinder, and carbapenemase genes were identified using ResFinder v.4.1 and CARD databases, requiring 100% identity and coverage.

Immunochromatographic assay protocol

The Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases assay is based on a nitrocellulose membrane pre-coated with antibodies-red polystyrene microspheres on five test lines against the five carbapenemases groups. In addition, rabbit polyclonal antibodies on an internal control line against a specific protein are included. Isolates grown for 18 h at 37°C on ChromID® CARBA and OXA-48 agar (bioMérieux) were processed according to the manufacturer’s instructions: five colonies were resuspended in extraction buffer, lysed (15 seconds), vortexed (15 seconds) and seven drops were dispensed onto the cassette. Results were read after 15 minutes: one red test band with the green internal control was considered positive; the presence of the internal control alone was considered negative. An external lyophilized positive control containing recombinant carbapenemase antigens was included. Negative results were re-tested with increased inoculum (up to 25 colonies) and in parallel with NG-Test CARBA-5 according to manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated with 95% CI, using vassarStats. The Youden’s index was calculated to assess overall performance.

Data availability

Genome sequences are available under BioProject accessions PRJNA1289100, https://dataview.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/object/PRJNA1289100?reviewer=aphe5gjdussr8b0ci609hht7mm and PRJNA1026089.

Results

Performance of carbapenemase-detection LFIA in bacterial isolates

Among the 251 carbapenemase-producing Gram-negative isolates, there were 57 KPC, 64 IMP, 63 VIM, 53 NDM and 68 OXA-like producers (Figure 1; Table S2), all submitted to the Andalusian Reference Laboratory. Species distribution included 121 Klebsiella pneumoniae complex, 109 Enterobacter spp., 48 Pseudomonas aeruginosa, 33 Citrobacter spp., 17 Escherichia coli, nine Klebsiella oxytoca, six Serratia marcescens, four Aeromonas spp. and single isolates of Acinetobacter spp., Pseudomonas stutzeri, Raoultella ornithinolytica and Providencia stuartii. The overall sensitivity and specificity of the test were 96.4% and 100%, respectively. The PPV and NPV resulted 100% and 91.7%, respectively (Table 1). No false-positive results were detected and all the non-carbapenemase producers were negative. No cross-reaction was observed with extended-spectrum β-lactamases (ESBLs) or AmpCs enzymes. The overall Youden index was 0.96, while it ranged for each carbapenemase group (Table 1). IMP and NDM variants were always detected (100% sensitivity). Some KPC, OXA-372-like and VIM variants showed reduced detection rates (93.0%, 95.6% and 96.8%, respectively (Table 1)).

Discussion

This evaluation demonstrates high accuracy of the novel Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases for detecting the five major carbapenemase groups using a large, multicentre and well-characterized WGS isolate collection. The assay achieved excellent performance across diverse species, enzyme variants and genetic lineages.

Overall sensitivity and specificity were comparable to other commercial LFIAs used in clinical microbiology laboratories, including NG-Test CARBA-5 and RESIST-4 O.K.N.V. In a previous comparative study (n = 152), NG-Test CARBA-5 and RESIST-4 O.K.N.V. showed sensitivities of 88.2% and 84.2%, respectively.2 By contrast, another evaluation reported higher values for NG-Test CARBA-5 (100% sensitivity and specificity) and RESIST-5 O.O.K.N.V. (99.4% sensitivity and 100% specificity), outperforming Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases.9 These discrepancies across studies highlight the importance of strain composition, variant distribution and methodological approaches.

Previous LFIA assays have reported limitations in detecting certain NDM and IMP producers. Early evaluations of RESIST-3 K-SeT described false-negative results in NDM-1-producing isolates,4,9–11 with detection sometimes improving using higher inoculum or zinc-enriched media.10 By contrast, all NDM producers in our study, including an NDM-1-producing P. stuartii isolate, were correctly detected with the recommended inoculum. Likewise, although reduced sensitivity for IMP detection has also been previously reported,12 all IMP variants included in our evaluation were correctly detected.

In our study, false-negatives were observed for OXA, KPC and VIM variants. OXA-1054, belonging to OXA-372-like group and identified from wastewater isolates,13 was not detected by Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases or NG-Test CARBA-5, consistent with previous reports for OXA-372-producing isolates.3,14,15 Conversely, other clinically relevant OXA-48-like variants (OXA-181 and OXA-244) were correctly detected. Occasional failures in detecting OXA-181 with NG-Test CARBA-5 have been described,16 whereas optimal detection was reported with the OXA-48 K-SeT assay.17 KPC-31 variant yielded negative results in our study, as previously reported with NG-Test CARBA-5 and RESIST K-SeT 5.18,19 However, unless other LFIAs have reportedly detected this variant, available data are based on only two isolates.19 Regarding VIM-63, no evaluation data with other LFIAs are available. In our study, KPC-31 and VIM-63 showed reduced detection with the standard inoculum, and increasing bacterial load restored weak positivity, suggesting that low enzyme expression or structural alterations may affect antibody recognition. No false-positives were observed in our study, but false-positive NDM bands with high inoculum have been described for NG-Test CARBA-5 and K.N.I.V.O K-SeT assays.20

Several factors may explain the false-negative results observed. Amino acid substitutions, such as the D179Y change in KPC-31 from KPC-3 and the A676C substitution of VIM-63 from VIM-2, may alter conformational epitopes and reduce antibody binding. In addition, low enzyme expression may generate insufficient antigen quantity for visible signal detection. Finally, as commercial LFIAs are primarily developed against epidemiologically prevalent variants, rare or emerging enzymes may escape detection. This could be the case for OXA carbapenemases other than OXA-48, such as OXA-1054 that belongs to the OXA-372 group, and may present difficulties for OXA recognition.

The main limitation of this assessment is the absence of certain carbapenemase variants, including OXA-48-like enzymes lacking carbapenemase activity. Accurate discrimination between OXA-48 and ESBL variants as OXA-163 and OXA-405 remains essential, as false-positive results have been previously reported.3 The isolates tested reflect our regional epidemiology, and variants circulating in other areas were not represented. Nevertheless, our panel included other rare and previously untested variants (OXA-1054 and VIM-63), expanding available performance data. The use of rabbit polyclonal antibodies may enhance detection of diverse carbapenemase variants by recognizing multiple epitopes. However, broader reactivity and batch-to-batch variability are potential limitations. In our study, no false-positives were observed, suggesting maintained specificity under the tested conditions. As serial dilution experiments of purified enzymes were not performed, therefore a limit of detection could not be determined. Our findings reflect the performance of the assay under routine laboratory conditions, using the inoculum recommended by the manufacturer, and should not be interpreted as defining the minimum detectable producer load. The principal strength of this work lies in the use of a large, multicentre and genomically well-characterized collection, ensuring accurate variant identification. By contrast, previous studies relied only on PCR or Sanger sequencing for characterization, which may provide less comprehensive genetic information.11

Conclusions

Certest ResisCheck® Carbapenemases assay demonstrated high accuracy for detecting major carbapenemase groups in clinical isolates harbouring one or more carbapenemases. It is an efficient, rapid and convenient diagnostic tool for detecting carbapenemases encountered in clinical Gram-negative bacteria, completing the existing panels of available tests for carbapenemase identification. As no current LFIA detects all variants, reliable identification of the most prevalent enzymes remains essential for timely therapeutic decisions and for the detection of the same resistant determinants transmission.
