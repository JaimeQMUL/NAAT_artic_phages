This study focuses on the structural basis of lipopolysaccharide O-antigen chain length modulation in a gram-negative bacterium using cryo-electron microscopy. The research details the structure and dynamics of the copolymerase component responsible for this modulation. The mechanism proposed is that the O-antigen chain length is determined by the internal volume of the copolymerase's periplasmic domain, which acts as a scaffold for polymerization. The analysis compares the structures of the copolymerase from the studied bacterium with a related homolog, revealing structural differences that correlate with the observed differences in O-antigen chain lengths.
