Concise Summary:
This review focuses on the structural, mechanistic, and catalytic diversity of nonheme iron enzymes, covering both mononuclear and dinuclear systems. The discussion centers on the chemical principles of controlled molecular oxygen activation, detailing how variations in the metal coordination sphere (e.g., 2-His-1-carboxylate vs. 3-His/4-His motifs) and the presence of cooperative metal centers (diiron systems) dictate the formation and reactivity of high-valent iron-oxygen intermediates (e.g., Fe(IV)=O, Fe(III)-superoxo, $\mu$-peroxo-diiron(III/III)). Key mechanistic insights are derived from studying specific enzyme classes: Thiol Dioxygenases (e.g., CDO), which utilize 3-His motifs and often show substrate-derived ligation; Sulfoxide Synthases, which catalyze oxidative C–S bond formation; and various dioxygenases (e.g., Dke1, GDO, CCOs), which demonstrate substrate-as-ligand versus substrate-in-second-sphere mechanisms. For diiron systems (HDOs), the iron-iron distance is highlighted as a critical structural determinant controlling multi-electron oxidation chemistry. The analysis emphasizes that the functional plasticity of these enzymes stems from their ability to tune redox potentials and intermediate stability through precise coordination geometry, rather than providing specific thermal adaptation data.
