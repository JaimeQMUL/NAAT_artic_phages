Paper: PMC12459788.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal lytic activity was observed between pH 5 and 9, and stability was assessed across temperatures up to 60°C.
- The study assessed stability at 25°C (room temperature), 37°C (normal body temperature), and 40, 50, 60, and 70°C.
- All phages remained stable and viable within the temperature range of 25°C to 60°C.
- Phages showed no lytic activity at 70°C, with titers decreasing by approximately 90% compared to 25°C.
- Three phages were noted to survive at 70°C, while five phages showed no lytic activity at this temperature.
- The stability up to 60°C suggests a degree of thermal resilience.

Environmental Context:
- Samples were collected from environmental sources including hospital sewages, wastewater, and soils.

Concise Summary:
The isolated phages demonstrated physiological stability across a range of temperatures, showing effective lytic activity up to 60°C. The optimal lytic activity was noted within the pH range of 5 to 9. The study assessed viability across 25°C to 70°C, confirming stability up to 60°C, although titers significantly decreased at higher temperatures. The phages were isolated from environmental reservoirs such as hospital sewage, wastewater, and soil. The findings indicate that the phages possess thermal resilience suitable for moderate temperature applications, with optimal activity observed near physiological conditions (37°C) and within the pH range of 5–9.
