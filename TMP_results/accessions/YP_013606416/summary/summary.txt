Paper: PMC10054541.1.txt
Thermal Evidence:
- Optimal growth temperature: 35 °C (for phage adsorption, one-step growth, and endolysin activity assays).
- Growth temperature range: Assays were conducted at 35 °C, with phage stability tested across a range from 5 °C to 95 °C.
- Survival temperature range: Phage stability was assessed up to 95 °C, with significant loss of titer observed at 65 °C (1000-fold reduction) and complete inactivation at 75 °C and higher.
- Incubation conditions: Endolysin activity was tested at 35 °C for 10 hours (turbidimetry) and thermal stability was tested at 20–70 °C for 1 hour.
- Heat tolerance: Endolysin Gp84 retained activity after incubation at 60 °C, while Gp82 showed significant loss of activity at 60 °C. Gp84 was completely inactivated at 70 °C.
- Cold tolerance: Phage titers remained similar to the control sample incubated at 5 °C, indicating stability at lower temperatures.
- Enzymes adapted to temperature: The thermal stability of endolysins was specifically tested, showing differential retention of activity across the tested temperature gradient.

Environmental Context:
- Habitat/Source: The phage was isolated from a commercial phage cocktail used in clinical settings. The host bacteria are associated with human infections (Enterococcus species).
- Extreme Environment Isolation: No evidence suggests isolation from an extreme environment.
- Explicit Classification: No explicit classification (e.g., mesophile, thermophile) was provided for the phage or its associated enzymes based on the thermal data presented.
